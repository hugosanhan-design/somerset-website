Find Your Match!

A mingling, comparative-adjective warm-up — famous athletes and historical figures, one secret character per student.
How to run itGive each of the 11 students one Character Card (cut from the sheets that follow) and one Mission Sheet. Nobody shows their card. Students walk around the room asking each other questions to find a classmate whose character matches each of the 15 clues. When they find a match, they write down: the classmate's name, their character's name, and the number or fact that proves it. A different classmate can confirm more than one clue.
Language to model first
  • Is your character taller than mine?
  • How many pets does your character have?
  • My character is Rafael Nadal.
  • Yes, he is! / No, she isn't.
Valencia / Spain connectionTwo cards are built in: Rafael Nadal trains not far from here, on the Spanish coast, and Christopher Columbus sailed for Isabel la Católica, Queen of Spain — also in the deck. Worth pointing out to the class once the mingling settles.
Honest note for you, not the classHeights for the athletes are roughly accurate. Everything else — pets, houses, cars, hair length, "bravery" — is invented for the game, the same way the Unit 1 Top Trumps deck uses made-up stats. No need to mention this to the students; it just keeps the numbers fun and comparable.
