Ten feelings, and the words for them

You already know what these feelings are. Today you get the exact English noun for each one, the adjective that goes with it, and the grammar to say what you are doing right now instead of what you usually do.

TASK 2One feeling, three wordson your own, then compare
The rule the exam tests. An adjective ending in -ed says how the person feels. An adjective ending in -ing says what caused it. I was bored is about you. The film was boring is about the film. Get these the wrong way round and the sentence says something you did not mean.

TASK 4What you do, and what you are doingpart A, on your own
Present simple for facts, permanent situations, habits and timetables. Present continuous for what is happening now, for temporary situations, for changing situations, for arrangements you have already made, and with always for a habit that annoys you. Some verbs, such as want, love, know and seem, do not normally take the continuous at all.
Put each verb in brackets into the present simple or the present continuous. There are eleven.

Hi Aidan,

Thanks for the photos. Shrewsbury (look) tiny next to Valencia, but I (love) that river.

Things here are strange at the moment. Normally I (get) the metro to school at half past seven, but they (repair) the line all this month, so I (walk) instead. It (take) me forty minutes, which is no disaster, because the Turia gardens (be) on the way.

My sister (stay) with us until December while her flat is finished. She (always, leave) her wet towels on my bed.

Anyway, our train to Barcelona (leave) at 7.40 on Friday, so I will not be online that morning. Write back before then. I (want) to hear about the new school.
