Unit 1, pages 8 and 9, in sixty minutes

Read this before the run order

This booklet is the Somerset half, not the book. The Student's Book is not on disk, so nothing here reproduces it. Open the book for the vocabulary and grammar boxes on pages 8 and 9, then use these five pages for the practice. Teacher's notes for this spread are at PDF pages 21 to 23 of Close_up_B2_Teachers_Book.md.

With two students there is no grouping decision. Everything is A and B. That also means nobody can hide, so the pace has to come from you, and silence lasts twice as long as it does in a group of six.

Víctor. The journal for 10 September says he finds it hard to speak confidently. Task 1 is built so he cannot dry up: the answer set is ten words on the page in front of him. In Task 4 part B his card carries the questions he has to ask, written out, so he never has to invent one cold. If he stalls anywhere, ask a prepared question rather than waiting: Which one is closest to you? · Say it again with the whole sentence. · Which word did you want there? Do not let a silence run in a class of two.

Martina. She learnt her English from series, so Task 1 and the speaking will come easily and the labelling will bore her. Her work is Task 2 and the letter, where the accuracy gap shows. If she finishes part A early, have her write next to each answer which of the six uses it is, in her own words. That is the piece she is missing, not the vocabulary.

The two without a neat single opposite are stubborn and sensitive. Accept any sensible phrase. Sensitive is the item to spend a minute on: positive when it means understanding other people's feelings, negative when it means easily upset. The book makes the same point on page 8.
