Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Grammar · FCE I · Unit 1

The basic idea

Habit or right now?

Present simple

+I walk to school.
She walks to school.
−I don't walk.
She doesn't walk.
?Do you walk?
Does she walk?

Present continuous

+I am walking.
She is walking.
−I am not walking.
She isn't walking.
?Am I walking?
Is she walking?

From your Student's Book, p. 9: "Helen lives alone in the city centre" (permanent) vs. "In this photo, two people are arguing" (right now).

When we use it

Present simple

  • Scientific facts and general truths"The adult human brain weighs about 1.3 kilograms."
  • Permanent situations"Helen lives alone in the city centre."
  • Habits and repeated actions"I go for a long walk every evening to relax."
  • Timetabled and scheduled events"The psychology lecture is at 12 o'clock every Monday this month."
  • Narratives — stories, sports commentaries, plots"At the end of the book, the man realises his mistake and apologises."

Typical signal words

alwaysusuallyoften neverevery dayon Tuesdays
When we use it

Present continuous

  • Happening at the moment"What are you thinking about right now?"
  • Temporary situations"This week, we're studying the possible causes of stress and anxiety."
  • Fixed arrangements for the future"My sister's getting married next month."
  • Annoying habits — with always, continually, forever"You're always telling me what to do!"
  • Changing situations — often with comparatives"I'm getting more and more optimistic about the future."
  • Describing what's happening in a picture"In this photo, two people are arguing."

Typical signal words

nowright nowat the moment currentlythese daysthis week
Common mistake

Some verbs don't go continuous

Your book's own rule (p. 9, Remember box): "Some verbs (stative verbs) are not usually used in a continuous form. These include verbs of emotion (e.g. hate, want) and verbs which express a state of mind (e.g. know, seem)."

State verbs to watch

hatewantknowseem like / lovebelieveunderstandneed
Watch out I'm hating this song. → I hate this song.
Sofia is wanting to leave early. → Sofia wants to leave early.
