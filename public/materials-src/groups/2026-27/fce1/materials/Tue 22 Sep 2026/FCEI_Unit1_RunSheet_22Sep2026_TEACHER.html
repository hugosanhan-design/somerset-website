2. Grammar: present simple vs present continuous · 25 min

Materials: FCEI_Unit1_Grammar_PresentSimpleContinuous_22Sep2026.html (5 slides, click through). Student's Book p.9, Exercise 1.

Slides (12 min). Habit-or-right-now contrast, present simple uses, present continuous uses, then the stative-verbs slide. On the stative-verbs slide, come back to item 1 from the warm-up: worry is usually stative (I worry about my exams), but it can take the continuous when it describes a temporary, changing situation rather than a general feeling, exactly the point the deck's "changing situations" use covers. Local version of the same pattern, for the mandatory Valencia reference: rising rents in Valencia are worrying young people in the city right now (continuous, a situation that's changing this year) versus Valencia has around 800,000 inhabitants (simple, a stable fact). Ask the group for one more real example each of a "changing situation" they've noticed in the city.

Exercise 1, p.9 (13 min). Thirteen verbs, present simple or continuous. Students complete individually in the book, check in a three (not pairs, per rule 3b, all three compare before anyone commits to an answer), then reveal with slide 5 of the deck, which carries the answer key on screen.
