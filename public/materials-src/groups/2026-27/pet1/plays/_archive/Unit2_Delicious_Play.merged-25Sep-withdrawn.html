A kitchen in Valencia. A grandmother, Iaia, is talking with her grandchildren about how things used to be.

Tableware, packaging & quantities · used to / would · be used to / get used to

Nora
Iaia, tell me about when you were young, what did the kitchen use to look like?
Iaia
We used to keep everything in jars and bottles, there was no fridge until I was ten.
Nora
Really? What did you use to drink water from?
Iaia
We used to drink from a jug, and we only had one cup each, no mugs like you have now.
Nora
That's so different! Did you use to share a bowl for everyone?
Iaia
We would share one big bowl of soup at the table, actually, with just one spoon each.
Nora
I'm used to having my own plate and bowl, I couldn't share like that.
Iaia
You'll get used to it if you have to, believe me. We used to buy rice in a big box too, not small packets like now.
Nora
And what about the market? Did you use to buy a bunch of anything?
Iaia
Every week, a bunch of grapes and a jar of honey. Things were simple, but we always had enough.
Nora
I love hearing about this, thank you Iaia.
Nora
Iaia, tell me about when you were young, what did the kitchen use to look like?
Iaia
We used to keep everything in jars and bottles, there was no fridge until I was ten.
Martí runs in holding a tin of tomatoes.
Martí
Iaia, what's this? Did you use to eat from tins like this one?

Nora
One spoon each? I'm used to having my own plate and bowl, I couldn't share like that.
Martí
Me neither! I'm getting used to sharing my toys, but not my food.
Iaia
You'll get used to it if you have to, believe me. We used to buy rice in a big box too, not small packets like now.
Nora

Iaia

Martí

Iaia
Of course, come on, let's go to the market together.

The annual paella contest in Sueca, in the Ribera del Júcar, during the judging.

Taste adjectives · cooking verbs · past simple & past continuous

Judge
Good morning! So, tell me, what were you doing when I arrived?
Marisa
I was stirring the rice when you walked in, sorry! I started cooking two hours ago.
Judge
No problem. What ingredients did you use today?
Marisa
I used chicken, rabbit, green beans and saffron, the traditional way.
Judge
And how did you cook the rice, exactly?
Marisa
I fried the meat first, then I boiled everything together for about twenty minutes.
Judge
Was anything difficult while you were cooking?
Marisa
Actually yes, while I was adding the saffron, the wind blew some ash from the fire into the pan! I had to start that part again.
The judge tastes a spoonful from the pan.
Judge
Mmm, this is delicious, not too salty at all.
Marisa
Thank you! I was worried it would be too spicy, I added a little pepper by accident.
Judge
No, it's perfect, nice and sweet from the tomato. Did you cook anything else today?
Marisa
Yes, while the rice was cooking, I grilled some vegetables for the judges to try as well.
Judge
Wonderful, very generous. While I was walking around earlier, I tasted twelve paellas, and yours is one of the best.
Marisa
Really? Thank you so much, that means a lot.
Judge
Good morning! So, tell me, what were you doing when I arrived?
Marisa
I was stirring the rice when you walked in, sorry! I started cooking two hours ago.
Pep, cooking at the next fire, leans over with his own spoon.
Pep
Sorry to interrupt, Judge, but you should try mine too when you're ready!
Judge
Of course, Pep, I'll come to you next. Marisa, what ingredients did you use today?
Marisa
I used chicken, rabbit, green beans and saffron, the traditional way.
Pep
I used exactly the same, actually, but I fried the meat for longer than Marisa did.
Judge
Interesting. Was anything difficult while you were cooking, either of you?
Marisa
While I was adding the saffron, the wind blew some ash from the fire into my pan! I had to start that part again.
Pep
That happened to me last year! This year I was more careful, luckily.
The judge tastes both pans.
Judge
Marisa's is delicious, not too salty. Pep, yours is nice and sweet from the tomato, but a little spicy for me.
Pep
I added extra pepper by accident, sorry!
Marisa
While the rice was cooking, I grilled some vegetables too, would you like to try them?
Judge
Wonderful, very generous, both of you. While I was walking around earlier, I tasted twelve paellas, and you're both in my top three.
Marisa & Pep
Thank you so much, that means a lot!

An evening cooking class at the Mercado de Colón in Valencia. Tonight's menu covers a bit of everything.

Cooking verbs & taste adjectives · tableware, packaging & quantities · past simple/continuous · used to / would

Chef Bruno
Right, everyone, tonight we're going to fry, boil and grill, all in one class. Ona, what were you doing when I called your name?
Ona
Sorry, I was tasting the sauce, it's a bit too sour, I think I used too much lemon.
Chef Bruno
Easy fix, add a spoon of sugar. First, though, pass me the jar of olive oil and a bowl for the vegetables.
Ona
Here you go. My grandmother used to cook like this every Sunday, she never used a recipe, just a jug of stock and her own taste.
Chef Bruno
That's how the best cooks learn. Right, while the rice is boiling, let's grill these vegetables. Can you pass me a plate, not a bowl?
Ona
Here. I'm not really used to grilling, I usually just fry or bake everything.
Chef Bruno
You'll get used to it fast, it's easier than frying. There, how does that taste?
Ona
Delicious, actually! Not salty, not bitter, just right. What was I doing wrong with the sauce earlier?
Chef Bruno
Nothing serious, you were just adding the lemon too fast. Now, pour it into that jug and let's serve it with the rice.
Ona
Thank you, Chef Bruno. I used to think cooking was boring, but this class is changing my mind completely.
Chef Bruno
Right, everyone, tonight we're going to fry, boil and grill, all in one class. Ona, what were you doing when I called your name?
Ona
Sorry, I was tasting the sauce, it's a bit too sour, I think I used too much lemon.
Dani arrives late, carrying a box of vegetables from the market stall outside.
Dani
Sorry I'm late! I was queuing for a bunch of herbs at the market, it took forever.
Chef Bruno
No problem, Dani, put the box down and pass me the jar of olive oil and a bowl for the vegetables.
Ona
Here you go. My grandmother used to cook like this every Sunday, she never used a recipe, just a jug of stock and her own taste.
Dani
Same in my house! We would always eat from one big bowl, not separate plates like this class uses.

Interesting, different families, different habits. Right, while the rice is boiling, let's grill these vegetables. Dani, pass me a plate, not a bowl?
Dani
Here. I'm still getting used to grilling, at home we only ever fried things.
Ona
Me too, I'm not really used to it either, I usually just bake everything.
Chef Bruno
You'll both get used to it fast, it's easier than frying. There, how does that taste?
Ona
Delicious, actually! Not salty, not bitter, just right.
Dani
Agreed, much better than what I was cooking at home last week, that came out far too spicy.
Chef Bruno
Good, now pour it into that jug and let's serve it with the rice, both of you.
Ona & Dani
Thank you, Chef Bruno, this class is changing our minds about cooking completely.
