The annual paella contest in Sueca, in the Ribera del Júcar, during the judging.

Taste adjectives · cooking verbs · past simple & past continuous

Judge
Good morning! So, tell me, what were you doing when I arrived?
Student B
I was stirring the rice when you walked in, sorry! I started cooking two hours ago.
Judge
No problem. What ingredients did you use today?
Student B
I used chicken, rabbit, green beans and saffron, the traditional way.
Judge
And how did you cook the rice, exactly?
Student B
I fried the meat first, then I boiled everything together for about twenty minutes.
Judge
Was anything difficult while you were cooking?
Student B
Actually yes, while I was adding the saffron, the wind blew some ash from the fire into the pan! I had to start that part again.
The judge tastes a spoonful from the pan.
Judge
Mmm, this is delicious, not too salty at all.
Student B
Thank you! I was worried it would be too spicy, I added a little pepper by accident.
Judge
No, it's perfect, nice and sweet from the tomato. Did you cook anything else today?
Student B
Yes, while the rice was cooking, I grilled some vegetables for the judges to try as well.
Judge
Wonderful, very generous. While I was walking around earlier, I tasted twelve paellas, and yours is one of the best.
Student B
Really? Thank you so much, that means a lot.
Judge
Good morning! So, tell me, what were you doing when I arrived?
Student B
I was stirring the rice when you walked in, sorry! I started cooking two hours ago.
Student C, cooking at the next fire, leans over with his own spoon.
Student C
Sorry to interrupt, Judge, but you should try mine too when you're ready!
Judge
Of course, Student C, I'll come to you next. Student B, what ingredients did you use today?
Student B
I used chicken, rabbit, green beans and saffron, the traditional way.
Student C
I used exactly the same, actually, but I fried the meat for longer than Student B did.
Judge
Interesting. Was anything difficult while you were cooking, either of you?
Student B
While I was adding the saffron, the wind blew some ash from the fire into my pan! I had to start that part again.
Student C
That happened to me last year! This year I was more careful, luckily.
The judge tastes both pans.
Judge
Student B's is delicious, not too salty. Student C, yours is nice and sweet from the tomato, but a little spicy for me.
Student C
I added extra pepper by accident, sorry!
Student B
While the rice was cooking, I grilled some vegetables too, would you like to try them?
Judge
Wonderful, very generous, both of you. While I was walking around earlier, I tasted twelve paellas, and you're both in my top three.
Student B & Student C
Thank you so much, that means a lot!

A fruit stall at the Mercado Central de Valencia, the morning after a cooking disaster.

Taste adjectives · cooking verbs · past simple & past continuous

Student A
You won't believe what happened yesterday! I was baking a cake for my mum's birthday when the oven stopped working.
Student B
Oh no, what did you do?
Student A
I called my neighbour, and while I was waiting for her, I roasted some vegetables in her oven instead, just to save the evening.
Student B
Good thinking! Did the cake taste OK in the end?
Student A
Actually, it was a bit sour, I think I used the wrong lemon. My mum said it was fine, but I could taste it clearly.
Student B
Ha! Well, at least the vegetables were good. Were you barbecuing outside too?
Student A
No, we didn't barbecue, it was raining all evening. What were you doing yesterday?
Student B
I was grilling fish at my grandparents' house when you called me, actually, that's why I didn't answer.
Student A
Oh, I wondered why! Was it good?
Student B
Really good, not salty at all, just perfect. Come on, let's buy some lemons here, the ones at this stall look fresh.
Student A
Good idea, mine tasted bitter, I definitely bought bad ones last time.

A video call the evening after an horchata-making workshop in Alboraia.

Taste adjectives · cooking verbs · past simple & past continuous

Student B
Amazing! While I was making the horchata, the teacher explained the whole history of Alboraia's chufa fields.
Student A
That's so interesting. What did you actually do?
Student B
We boiled the water first, then we mixed it with the chufa. I also tried baking some fartons to go with it.

Very sweet, perfect with the horchata. Someone dropped a tray of them while we were cleaning up, it was chaos for a minute.
Student A

I was washing some glasses when I heard the noise, I turned around and there was sugar everywhere.
Student A
Was the horchata good in the end?

Delicious, not too sweet, not sour at all, just right. You should come next time.
