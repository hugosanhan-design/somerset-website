Reading / 5

Read the text about an interesting job.

The coolest job in the world!

What's it like to dive a thousand feet down in a small submarine in the ocean around the Galápagos Islands? National Geographic Explorer Jessica Cramp takes us on a journey to find out.

Jessica works on a project with National Geographic and Ecuador's government. 1 ‘We need to make sure that we understand what's down there, so that we can work hard to protect it,’ Jessica explains. On this dive, she is exploring the deep waters of Punta Vicente Roca. She is diving with two other women, the producer Maureen Dolan-Galaviz and submarine pilot Diana Garcia-Benito. 2

It's not easy to go so deep in such a small submarine because you are locked in a very small space for hours. 3 ‘There is no getting out once you're down 400 metres (1,312 feet),’ Jessica says.

On the way down, they see lots of sharks. 4 They also see other sea animals such as sea lions and swordfish. When you are that deep, it's usually very dark, so a submarine with bright lights makes the animals curious.

5 ‘I have the coolest job in the world,’ she says. This is especially true when she's deep down in the ocean. ‘I've never felt as much like an explorer than I do right now in the submarine. There's something about being a thousand feet under the water, where no one else has actually ever been, that feels, I don't know, like true exploration.’
