Present Simple, Present Continuous and Stative Verbs

Grammar · PET I · Unit 1

The basic idea

Habit or right now?

Present simple

+I work.
She works.
−I don't work.
She doesn't work.
?Do you work?
Does she work?

Present continuous

+I am working.
She is working.
−I am not working.
She isn't working.
?Am I working?
Is she working?

Cristina works at the Mercado de Ruzafa every Saturday (her normal job). This week she's covering a stall at the Central Market for a friend who's ill (just this week).

When we use it

Present simple

  • Facts and general truths"Wood comes from trees."
  • Permanent situations"Pat works for the council."
  • Habits and repeated actions"I swim every day."
  • Future events based on schedules"Our train leaves at 2 p.m."
  • Narratives, stories, sports commentaries, plots"The Happy Prince smiles."

Frequency

When we use it

Present continuous

  • Actions in progress right now"I'm having dinner now."
  • Temporary or changing situations"I'm studying to be a vet. Tim is growing up fast."
  • Annoying habits"He's always complaining!"
  • Fixed arrangements for the future"We're moving next week."
  • Describing what's happening in a picture"Jim and I are walking around in Rome."

Common mistake

Some verbs don't go continuous

Emotions

hate, like, love, need, prefer, want

Senses

feel, hear, see, smell, sound, taste

Mind

believe, forget, know, remember, think, understand

Possession

belong, have, own

stative Jim is very silly. (usual behaviour)
action Amy is just being silly. (not her normal behaviour)
stative I have a great bike. (own)
action He's having dinner. (eat)
stative I think this is great. (opinion)
action I'm thinking about him. (have on your mind)
stative This lasagna smells great. (has a smell)
action The cat's smelling the food. (sniffing it)

Some verbs, e.g. like, hate, be, belong and know, are stative verbs. They describe states, not actions. We don't use these verbs in continuous forms.
