1. Warmer & revision · 8 min

Materials: none, whiteboard only.

PET I already has present simple from previous levels; this warmer activates it before Unit 1 pushes them into present continuous and stative verbs alongside it, so today's grammar lands as a contrast rather than something new from zero.

Write six prompts on the board (adapt live if a name doesn't fit):

  1. Find someone who takes the metro to Somerset.
  2. Find someone who has a part-time job or helps at home for money.
  3. Find someone who plays a sport on Saturdays.
  4. Find someone who wants to work in Valencia when they finish school.
  5. Find someone who hates getting up early.
  6. Find someone who is wearing something green today.

Prompts 1–5 are present simple, prompt 6 is present continuous, planted deliberately so students notice the odd one out when you ask “which sentence is different, and why?” at the end. That question is the bridge into Block 3.

2. Vocabulary: jobs & personality adjectives · 15 min

Materials: Booklet pages 1–2 (Exercises A & B). Teacher key on Booklet TEACHER.

Exercise A: Jobs. Match the 12 jobs from SB p8 to their definitions (Booklet). Students work alone, then check in pairs. Elicit which jobs exist at Somerset's own neighbourhood: a librarian at the Biblioteca Municipal, a firefighter on call during Fallas, a cook at a stall in the Mercado Central.

Exercise B: Personality adjectives. Gap-fill with the 18 adjectives, teacher's book pairs (generous/hard-working, nervous/relaxed, sociable/unkind, reliable/lazy, confident/patient, miserable/cheerful, dangerous/safe, calm, honest/dishonest, shy). Feedback: ask which adjectives suit which of today's jobs (a firefighter needs to be calm and reliable, an actor needs to be confident).

3. Grammar: present simple vs present continuous, stative verbs · 20 min

Materials: PETI_LevelLadder_Results_16Sep2026.pptx (class slides only), Booklet pages 2–4 (Grammar spotlight, Exercises C, D, E). Teacher key on Booklet TEACHER.

Updated 16 Sep after grading the Level Ladder: treat this block as first exposure, not review. 4 of 5 students missed diagnostic item 4 (She usually cycles, but this week she is taking the metro), only Álvaro got it.

Results + noticing (6 min). Show the class slides of the results deck, title through the Unit 1 slide (about 2 minutes, keep the per-student cards for private use at the end). Then turn to the Grammar spotlight on Booklet page 2: read item 4 aloud, let them vote, and build the two columns with them before they look at the table. Link to the warmer: I take the metro every day vs I'm wearing green today. Finish with the stative-verb box.

Controlled practice (9 min). Exercise C: Usually… but this week, 10 items, six of them habit-versus-this-week contrasts built from the students' own Task 1 answers (Dragon Ball, basketball, shopping centre, painting). Item 7 is the diagnostic sentence again, check who gets it now. Item 9 is a trap: have = eat takes -ing. Exercise D: stative-verb correction, five wrong, two correct.

Freer practice (5 min). Exercise E: three sentences (a habit, something different this week, a dream job with a stative verb), then said aloud in the trio and the pair. Each student reports one partner: My partner usually…, but this week…. Listen for the third-person -s and the missing is. Suggested groups: split the Maya, Valeria, Alejandra friendship trio and put Alejandra with Álvaro and Sergio, so the two lowest scorers are not a pair on their own.

4. Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 15 min

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit pet1-u01.

Open the Arcade, pick class PET I, unit pet1-u01. Set up Teams first: 2 teams (a 3 and a 2, handicap on so the pair isn't punished), paste the five names, Shuffle.

Play Point Grab straight through the 20-tile grid, all 20 questions are today's jobs, personality adjectives, present simple/continuous and stative verbs, mixed. Two tiles STEAL, two are ×2, exactly as usual. This is the review that puts today's whole lesson back in front of them once, out loud, competitively, before the board game asks them to produce it themselves.

Note: this unit file ships with vocab and questions only. cards (Memory Match / Guess Who) and case (the escape room) are not built yet, they need unit-specific photos first: see the note at the end of this run sheet. Point Grab, Speed Round, Word Flash, Hangman, Pilota, La Paella and Sambori all work today regardless, since they only read questions/vocab.

5. Board game: El Mercat de los Trabajos · 15 min

Materials: BoardGame sheet (landscape, one page for the 32-square board + scoreboard, two pages of cards), printed and cut, a die per group, a token per student, three card piles (Job, Grammar, Personality) shuffled face down.

Split into the trio and the pair, each group is one team on 0 points. The board runs 1 to 32 around a stylised Mercado Central, from the Cabanyal fish stalls to the clock tower. Card squares (green) draw from the matching pile, a correct answer scores +10, wrong answers score nothing and the group corrects together, no teacher marking mid-game. The board is livelier than a plain path now: gold squares double the next card's points, red squares cost 10 points outright, purple squares let a team steal 10 points from whoever is currently leading, teal squares hand out a free +15, and the single pink square lets a team swap their whole score with any other team, so it stays worth playing even for a team that's behind.

The game ends the moment a team reaches square 32 (worth +20). The winner is the team with the most points at that moment, not necessarily the team that arrived first, so don't let the trio assume it's over just because they got there first, check the scoreboard.

6. Play: The interview at the Ciutat · 15 min

Materials: Play script, Booklet back pages. Five roles, one per student, no doubling needed with exactly 5.

A short scripted scene: a job interview for an English-speaking guide role at the Ciudad de las Artes y las Ciencias, one interviewer and three candidates (an astronaut-exhibit guide, a camera operator for the planetarium, a hairdresser turned events host) plus one narrator who reads the stage directions and time expressions aloud. The script is dense with today's target language: jobs, personality adjectives, present simple facts about each candidate and present continuous lines for what's happening in the room, plus 3 stative verbs planted in the dialogue.

Give students 3 minutes silent read-through with a highlighter for any word they don't know, answer those quickly, then read it once through in role. If time allows, swap two roles and run the strongest scene (the interviewer + astronaut-guide exchange) a second time for pace and expression rather than accuracy.

7. Wrap-up · 2 min

Round the room: one sentence each, a job they'd hate and why, forcing a stative verb without a rule box on the board (I hate being a firefighter because I don't like danger.). No writing, just said aloud as they pack up.
