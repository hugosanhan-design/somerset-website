First day, 90 minutes

One change to your plan, and why

You asked for the solo presentation first and the mingle after. This reverses them. At fourteen and fifteen the audience that matters is not you, it is the other four, and a peer audience is the most threatening one there is at that age. Asked to stand and speak in the first fifteen minutes they give you their shortest possible answer and sit down, and day one has taught them that speaking here is a risk. Mingling first is loud and pairwise, so by the time they stand up they have already spoken English to everyone in the room.

The authority signal does not come from exposure. It comes from every instruction given once, in English, with a time limit you enforce. The stand-up still happens, all five, no opt out. They are simply set up to succeed at it rather than freeze.

Also changed: the 40-word message on Level Ladder page 3 is now a fast-finisher only, since Task 3 is a better writing sample and 90 minutes will not hold both.

And presentations are one minute, not ninety seconds. The profile no longer asks for a secret either: a fourteen-year-old asked to reveal something private to four strangers says nothing or says too much. It now asks what surprises people about them, which they can answer at whatever depth they choose.

Who is confident, and who is not

How to use this

Fill it in during the stand-up, not afterwards. You are recording behaviour, not marking English. A student who speaks for 90 seconds in poor English is more use to you this year than one who says four perfect sentences and sits down. Quiet and unwilling need opposite treatment, so keep them apart in your notes.
