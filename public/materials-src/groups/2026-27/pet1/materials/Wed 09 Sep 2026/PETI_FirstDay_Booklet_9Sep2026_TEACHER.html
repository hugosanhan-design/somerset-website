First day, 90 minutes

One change to your plan, and why

Mingle before solo. At fourteen and fifteen the audience that matters is not you, it is the other four, and asked to present cold they give you their shortest answer and sit down. After the mingle they have already spoken English to everyone in the room. The stand-up still happens, all five, no opt out. The authority signal comes from every instruction given once, in English, with a time limit you enforce, not from exposure.

Your new opening task replaced two blocks rather than adding a third. The twelve questions on page 1 are the profile, and asking those same twelve questions round the room on page 2 is the mingle, so the generic Find Someone Who is gone. Same mechanic, real matches instead of invented ones, and twelve minutes saved.

Also: presentations are one minute, not ninety, and the 40-word message on page 7 is a fast-finisher only.

Who is confident, and who is not

How to use this

Fill it in during the stand-up, not afterwards. You are recording behaviour, not marking English. A student who speaks for 90 seconds in poor English is more use to you this year than one who says four perfect sentences and sits down. Quiet and unwilling need opposite treatment, so keep them apart in your notes.

The Level Ladder, key and diagnosis

What this is measuring

Six parts, one per unit of New Close-up B1 Split A. The class has not studied any of it yet, so a low total is expected and is not the point. Each part is four grammar items they could plausibly have met at school and two vocabulary items from the unit's own word list. The grammar scores tell you what you can move through quickly. The vocabulary scores are only a rough signal, since the book's glossary is new to them.

Marks that are worth a second look. Q26 and Q27 both look like futures but split on offer versus evidence, and Q28 is an arrangement, so a student who puts will in all three has one rule, not three. Q13 and Q14 separate present perfect from past simple, which is the single most common Spanish-speaker error in this book. Q34 is the only question above the Split A syllabus, since second conditional appears at the end of Unit 6, so treat it as a bonus rather than a gap.
