Ainhoa Sonia Lucía (guest)

A café terrace in Ruzafa, three weeks after summer camp ended.

Present perfect simple & continuous · phrasal verbs · prepositions (p.13)

Ainhoa
I've missed you both so much! I haven't seen you since the summer camp.
Sonia
I know! I've been thinking about you all week actually — I've only just got back from my aunt's in Alicante.
Lucía
Wait, have you two fallen out or something? You've been weird with each other since June.
Ainhoa
No! Actually we've hit it off more than ever this summer — we've been messaging every day.
Sonia
True. Although her little brother has stood me up for a video call three times this week, so I've kind of gone off him.
Lucía laughs and picks up the menu.
Lucía
Classic. Anyway — has anyone read anything interesting these holidays? I've just finished a book about music and the brain. Apparently when you concentrate on a new song, your brain is constantly predicting what's coming, and getting it right can cheer you up instantly.
Ainhoa
That's amazing — I've never really thought about it before. Sharing stuff like that really leads to stronger friendships, don't you think?
Sonia
Definitely. I nearly burst into tears at a concert this summer for no reason — maybe that's why!
Lucía
Right, I haven't eaten since breakfast and it's getting me down. Shall we order?
stops at the table, a little shyly, with a tote bag over her shoulder.
Hi, sorry to interrupt — is this seat free? I've only just moved to Valencia and I haven't found a proper café yet.
Ainhoa
Of course, sit down! I'm Ainhoa, this is Sonia and that's Lucía. Have you been living here long?
Only two weeks. I've never seen so many people chilling out on a terrace in October! Honestly, the move got me down at first, but this is cheering me up already.
Sonia
Then stay with us. We hit it off with this place the very first week — you'll love it.
Lucía
And we promise not to stand you up, !
Ainhoa
Let's chill out here a bit first. We haven't had a proper catch-up in ages.
Elena Marta Valeria Zoe (guest)

Cycling along the Turia gardens on a Sunday afternoon.

Present perfect simple & continuous · phrasal verbs · prepositions (p.13)

Elena
I can't believe it's been three weeks since school finished — I've missed the Turia gardens so much.
Marta
Me too! Zoe, have you ever cycled here before? It's basically the whole city on one bike path.
Zoe
Never — I only moved here in September, remember? But I've already fallen in love with it. Everyone's been so welcoming, we hit it off straight away.
Valeria
Aw, we're glad you joined! Although Elena's been getting me down all summer complaining about her phone.
Elena
Because it broke and I still haven't fixed it! I've been relying on Marta for everything.
Marta
True, and honestly it's starting to get me down too, ha. Anyway, has anyone read that article about music and the brain?
Zoe
Yes! It said your brain concentrates on predicting the next note, and when it's right, it gives you a little reward — that's why a good song can cheer you up instantly.
Valeria
That explains why I nearly burst into tears at the fireworks concert in July — the music just got to me.
pulls up next to the group on a hired bike, a paper map in her hand.
Excuse me — is this the way to the Ciudad de las Artes? I've only just arrived and I've been going round in circles for an hour.
Elena
You're on the right path! We're going that way ourselves — I've cycled here a hundred times.
Thank goodness. I've never been to Valencia before, and getting lost has been getting me down all morning.
Marta
Cheer up! Come with us. Zoe only joined us in September and we hit it off with her in five minutes.
Valeria
Just don't stand us up at the end, — and don't ask Elena to fix your phone!
Elena checks her watch.
Elena
Sharing stuff like that really leads to stronger friendships, I think. Right, shall we get going before it gets dark?
Marta
Wait — don't stand us up like last time! You said that in June and then you never showed up.
Elena
I promise! I haven't stood anyone up since… well, since June.
Teacher's note (not for students). Two full, different scripts — not the same scene with names swapped — because the group is now seven (Zoe joined 25 Sep 2026): the established trio (Ainhoa, Sonia, Lucía) performs one, and the group of four (Elena, Marta, Valeria, Zoe) performs the other, so both groups run their own play in the same class rather than repeating one script. Both versions drill present perfect simple/continuous with the SB p.12 adverbs (already, still, yet, just, ever, never, before) and all seven p.13 phrasal verbs (go off, cheer up, chill out, hit it off, fall out, get down, stand up) plus four of the p.13 prepositions (lead to, concentrate on, responsible for → implied via "relying on", burst into). The Valorie Salimpoor open-cloze passage (music predicting the brain's reward response) is referenced in both scenes as a natural bridge into the Exam Task, paraphrased rather than quoted. Zoe's line in the quartet places her as an established member of the group, not a guest.
