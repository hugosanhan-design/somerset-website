Teacher run sheet · Friday B2 · 11 September 2026 · 15:45 to 17:45

First day, 120 minutes

Read this before the run order

Mingle before solo, and the Ladder last. A diagnostic in the first half hour sets a test tone on day one and tells them this year is about marks. Put the speaking first, the Ladder in the quiet middle-to-late stretch, and open the book before they leave so the year starts with the book, not with a score.

The risk with this group is not shyness. Six students who may already know each other will settle into Spanish and into a pecking order within twenty minutes if you let them. The defence is the language box on page 2, taught before the mingle and enforced during it, and the rule that they must collect a name from every person in the room, not just the friend next to them.

The six lines on page 3 are a B2 writing sample, not an icebreaker. Each frame carries a structure the book will test: a relative clause, a non-defining relative, present perfect continuous, present continuous against present simple, and a second conditional. Collect the booklet at the end. What they can and cannot finish there is worth more than the Ladder score.

Who is confident, and who is not

How to use this

Fill it in during the stand-up, not afterwards. You are recording behaviour, not marking English. At B2 the useful distinction is not fluent against hesitant, it is reaching against playing safe: a student who attempts a relative clause and mangles it is a better bet for June than one who delivers six flawless simple sentences. Quiet and unwilling need opposite treatment, so keep them apart in your notes.

The Level Ladder, key and diagnosis

What this is measuring

Six parts, one per unit of New Close-up B2 Split A. The class has not studied any of it yet, so a low total is expected and is not the point. Each part is four grammar items and two vocabulary items from the unit's own topic set. The grammar scores tell you what you can move through quickly. The vocabulary scores are a rough signal only, since the book's glossary is new to them. Several items are deliberate B2 discriminators rather than B1 revision: 9 and 10 separate used to from be used to, 14 and 15 separate past perfect simple from continuous, 27 is a perfect modal, and 32 turns on stop plus infinitive of purpose. Those four are the ones worth looking at first.
