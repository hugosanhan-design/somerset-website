Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can use a range of adjectives (in the correct order) to write a restaurant-review article of about 100 words, planned in three paragraphs, and can extract specific facts and figures from a short documentary video.
Target language. Adjective order and range (food, service, decor, price); planning an article into paragraphs; food-truck industry vocabulary (average, earnings, catering, bring in).
Exam link. B1 Preliminary Writing Part 2 (the 100-word article) is written for real today, individually, in class — never as homework.
Writing is always done in class, never homework. Students write the real Exam Task (about 100 words) individually under exam-like conditions and it is collected at the end for marking. The corrective slides, Arcade round and per-student Fix-It Sheets follow next class (l06), per the group's standard error-review sequence.
Materials: Student's Book pp.26-28. Video: "Food truck nation" (SB p.28; script and clip via Somerset laptop). 2–5. Writing: using adjectives, planning & organisation · 35 min

Materials: Student's Book pp.26-27, Ex.1–8.

Ex.1: three of six phrases are wrong because they break the order rule or stack too many adjectives (fix: "blue nice" → "nice blue"; "Chinese fantastic" → "fantastic Chinese"; "modern interesting" → "interesting modern"). Ex.2: choose the correct adjective in eight sentences about food and drink. Ex.3: sort ten adjectives (cheap, comfortable, delicious...) into food/service/decor/price. Ex.4: read the "Articles Wanted" notice and mark five statements true or false. Ex.5: choose the best option (1–8) to complete "Local restaurants", the model answer. Ex.7: complete Fernando's plan (birthday, near cinema, Mexican, enchiladas/spicy, colourful, friendly, delicious/cheap). Ex.8: sort the Useful Language adjectives into positive, negative, and either.

Ex.1: 1 nice blue · 2 ✓ · 3 fantastic Chinese · 4 interesting modern · 5 ✓ · 6 ✓.
Ex.2: 1 delicious · 2 burnt · 3 spicy · 4 tasty, colourful · 5 processed · 6 grilled · 7 cold · 8 sparkling.
Ex.3: food: delicious, spicy, tasty · service: fast, friendly, polite · decor: comfortable, modern, traditional · price: cheap, expensive, inexpensive.
Ex.4: 1T · 2F · 3T · 4T · 5T.
Ex.5: 1 inexpensive · 2 friendly · 3 popular · 4 tasty · 5 favourite · 6 different · 7 excellent · 8 special.
Ex.7: 1 Mexican · 2 near cinema · 3 birthday · 4 enchiladas, spicy · 5 colourful · 6 friendly · 7 delicious, cheap.
Ex.8: Positive: colourful, delicious, healthy, tasty, bright, charming. Negative: bitter, burnt, dull, tasteless, unhealthy, dirty, messy, rude, slow, unfriendly. Either: modern, old-fashioned, raw.

Valencia link: the Exam Task asks students to write about a restaurant they've visited — steer them towards somewhere real: a horchateria in Alboraia, a paella restaurant in El Palmar by the Albufera, a bar in Ruzafa, or a market stall at the Mercado Central. Real detail (what they ordered, the decor, the price) makes a stronger 100 words than an invented restaurant.

6–7. Exam Task: writing an article · 30 min

Materials: exam-task box, p.27. Plain paper or a printed template.

Task: "You see this notice on an English-language website. Articles Wanted: RESTAURANTS. Write about a restaurant you have visited recently. Did you enjoy the food? Would you recommend it? Write your answer in about 100 words." Students plan three paragraphs (as Ex.7 modelled), then write individually. Collect every script at the end for marking — this feeds directly into l06's writing error-review and the personalised Fix-It Sheets.

8. Video: Food truck nation · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.28. Video clip + factsheet.

Before you watch: discuss Los Angeles and food trucks (how do you order? pay at the truck, take your food away). While you watch: complete the facts-and-figures sheet with the numbers given (50, 236, 5,000, 43,000, 75,000, 5 million, 828 million). After you watch: complete the summary with the word box (catering, costs, quality, home-cooked, range, reasonably, social media, tweets), check on a second viewing.

Facts and figures: earnings of the US food truck industry $828 million · average cost of a truck $43,000 · total cost to get one ready for business $75,000 · % of adult Americans who've eaten from a food truck 50% · number of trucks sending tweets 5,000 · number of tweets sent 5 million · number of cities where trucks were active 236.
Summary (Ex.3): 1 home-cooked · 2 catering · 3 costs · 4 range · 5 quality · 6 reasonably · 7 social media · 8 tweets.

Video script (from the Teacher's Book, for the teacher's reference if the clip can't play): "Food trucks are everywhere now and people love their home-cooked food and low prices. In 2014, the food truck industry in the US earned $828 million. One reason for the success of the industry is the low cost of starting a business. For example, the average price for a truck is $43,000; add a logo and design, electronics, city permits, cooking supplies and ingredients, and the cost is around $75,000 to get a food truck ready for business... Food trucks use social media like Twitter and Facebook to let people know where they are, and to bring in new customers... In 2014 more than 5,000 trucks sent nearly five million tweets to their customers in 236 US cities."

9. Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 10 min

Materials: laptop + projector, unit b1a-u02-l05.

Built from the video's vocabulary and figures, and the Useful Language adjectives for reviewing a restaurant, since this page teaches a writing skill and video content rather than a wordlist.

Fast finisher

Fast finishers who finish the article early write one extra sentence recommending (or not) the restaurant to a friend, using "I would/wouldn't recommend..." from the Useful Language box.

If short of time, cut:

The carry-over play (block 1) first. If the video runs long, skip the after-you-watch summary gap-fill and give students the answers directly — do not cut the writing task itself or shorten its 25 minutes; a rushed exam-condition script is worse than a slightly shorter video segment.
