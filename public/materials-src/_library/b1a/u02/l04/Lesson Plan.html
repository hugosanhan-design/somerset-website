Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can name tableware, packaging and quantity words (bottle, jar, bunch, slice, packet, tin, etc.), and can choose accurately between used to/would and be used to/get used to.
Target language. Tableware & quantities vocabulary; used to and would for past habits/states; be used to (familiar) vs get used to (becoming familiar) + -ing/noun.
Exam link. Quantity/container collocations reappear in B1 Preliminary Reading Part 5/6 word-formation and gap-fill tasks; used to is a recurrent Use of English transformation item.
Materials: Student's Book pp.24-25. Audio: Track 2.6 (Ex.4, breakfast conversation). Answers_VISTA.html. Somerset Arcade: b1a-u02-l04. Play 2 at the end, if there's time: Unit2_Delicious_TablewareAndUsedTo_Play.html (tableware & used to).

2–4. Vocabulary: tableware, packaging & quantities · 35 min

Materials: Student's Book p.24, Ex.1–4. Track 2.6.

Ex.1: name the objects in the breakfast-table photo (plate, fork, knife, saucer, cup) and the fridge/pantry photo (jug, jars, bottle, eggs, cheese), then discuss five questions about jugs/jars/bottles/bowls. Ex.2: choose the correct option in eight sentences (bottle/box, fork/knife, cups/jars...). Ex.3: cross out the wrong word in six container phrases. Ex.4: complete the Omar/Amal breakfast dialogue with the eight words in the box, then check with Track 2.6.

Ex.2: 1 bottle · 2 fork · 3 jars · 4 forks · 5 cups, saucers, jug · 6 box · 7 plates · 8 bowl · 9 spoon.
Ex.3: 1 pasta · 2 bread · 3 strawberries · 4 milk · 5 rice · 6 pizza (the crossed-out word in each set).
Ex.4: 1 slices · 2 box · 3 bunch · 4 bowl · 5 bottles · 6 jar · 7 knife · 8 glasses.

Valencia link: ask what container horchata comes in at a horchateria in Alboraia (a glass, or a bottle to take away) and what quantity word goes with a Valencian dish like a "paella pan" (not a bowl). One student per pair names a food and its usual container.

5–8. Grammar: used to, would, be/get used to · 35 min

Materials: Student's Book p.25, Ex.1–4.

Ex.1: complete two rules (a: used to for states or repeated actions; b: would for repeated actions only, never states). Ex.2: tick which of eight sentences allow would instead of used to — the trap items are the ones describing states (own a microwave, cost less), which cannot take would. Ex.3: read the be/get used to sentences and answer which is already familiar (a) and which is becoming familiar (b). Ex.4: choose a/b/c for eight sentences mixing all three structures.

Ex.1: a used to · b would.
Ex.2 (tick): 1, 2, 5, 8 (repeated actions: prepare breakfast, eat rice, have barbecues, throw food — all can take would). Items 3, 4, 6, 7 cannot (a question form, a negative state, a price/state, "be" itself).
Ex.3: a1 (already usual/familiar) · b2 (new, becoming familiar).
Ex.4: 1a · 2c · 3c · 4b · 5a · 6c · 7c · 8b.

Valencia link: ask an Erasmus-in-Valencia question on the board: "When you first came to Spain, what were you getting used to?" (late dinner times, the siesta, everyone eating out on the terrace) vs "What are you used to now, after a year here?" One sentence each, in pairs.
