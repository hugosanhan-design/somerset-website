Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can use taste adjectives (bitter, sweet, sour, spicy, salty) and cooking verbs (bake, barbecue, boil, fry, grill, roast) accurately, and can choose correctly between the past simple and past continuous to narrate and give background.
Target language. Taste adjectives; cooking verbs; past simple vs past continuous (sequence of finished actions vs an action in progress, interrupted actions, background information).
Exam link. Multiple-choice cloze (Use of English style) reappears at B1 Preliminary Reading Part 5; the past simple/continuous contrast recurs in every narrative writing and listening task this year.
Materials: Student's Book pp.20-21. Answers_VISTA.html (reveal deck, all exercises from both pages). Somerset Arcade: b1a-u02-l02. Review play (if time): plays/Unit2_Delicious_TasteAndPast_Play.html.

2. Vocabulary: taste & cooking verbs · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.20, Ex.1–3.

Ex.1: elicit a few sweet/salty foods first, then do item 1 together (bitter, because sugar makes bitter foods taste sweeter). Ex.2: six photos (fried eggs, toast, boiled potatoes, grilled vegetables, roast chicken, baked cakes) — students write the cooking verb under each. Ex.3: choose the correct verb for each sentence (lamb chops, pasta water, birthday cake, steak, potatoes).

Ex.1: 1 bitter · 2 spicy · 3 Salty · 4 sweet · 5 sour.
Ex.2: 1 fry · 2 grill · 3 boil · 4 barbecue · 5 roast · 6 bake.
Ex.3: 1 barbecue · 2 boil · 3 bake · 4 fry, grill · 5 roast, boil.

Valencia link: ask which of these verbs describes how you cook paella (boil the rice, but the socarrat at the bottom is closer to fry) and horchata (neither — it's a cold drink, made by soaking and grinding tiger nuts). One student per group names a dish from home and the verb that cooks it.

Exam Task: multiple-choice cloze · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.20, Ex.4.

Skim first for the general topic (where the hamburger came from), then work gap by gap: look at the word before and after each gap, and check the whole sentence still makes sense. Two competing origin stories, six gaps.

Answers: 1 A (popular) · 2 C (rode) · 3 B (without) · 4 B (fried) · 5 B (added) · 6 A (returned).

4–5. Grammar: past simple · 20 min

Materials: Student's Book p.21, Ex.1–2.

Ex.1: match three sentences to their use (b actions one after the other; c a state/situation finished in the past; a a repeated past routine). Ex.2: complete "A giant pizza!" with the past simple form of the six verbs given.

Ex.1: 1b · 2c · 3a.
Ex.2: 1 started · 2 worked · 3 was · 4 used · 5 ate · 6 gave.

5–6. Grammar: past continuous & the contrast · 20 min

Materials: Student's Book p.21, Ex.3–5.

Ex.3: match four sentences to their use (a a single action in progress; b two actions in progress together; c an action interrupted; d background information). Flag the REMEMBER box: stative verbs never take the continuous (we don't say "I was knowing"). Ex.4: build four full past continuous sentences from prompts. Ex.5: choose past simple or continuous for six sentences — the real contrast test.

Ex.3: 1a · 2b · 3c · 4d.
Ex.4: 1 A: What were you doing at one o'clock on Monday? B: I was eating my lunch and listening to the radio. · 2 This time last week we were grilling burgers in my aunt's garden. · 3 Candice wasn't dancing at the party because she was serving food to the guests. · 4 Maria and Alfred were arguing about the best pizza toppings for hours last night.
Ex.5: 1 finished, ate · 2 Did Ludmila call, were watching · 3 cooked, was · 4 were making, walked · 5 didn't fry, grilled · 6 was tidying, was helping.

Valencia link: board example for Ex.5's contrast — "I was queuing at the Mercado Central when the paella stall sold its last pan" (interrupted action) vs "We finished the tour and then we ate lunch in Ruzafa" (sequence). Ask each student for one sentence about something that happened while they were doing something else in Valencia.
