Delicious! taste, cooking & the past

Tap "Show answers" on each slide. Eight exercises, in book order.

Vocabulary Ex.1 · taste

Choose the best adjective

  • 1This coffee is very bitter.
  • 2This curry is very spicy.
  • 3Salty snacks, like crisps, aren't very healthy.
  • 4I love sweet foods like cake and chocolate.
  • 5Yoghurt and lemon are sour foods.
Why: sugar makes bitter foods taste sweeter, which is why item 1 pairs coffee with "some sugar, please".
Vocabulary Ex.2-3 · cooking verbs

Name the cooking method

  • 1Fried eggs → fry
  • 2Toast under the grill → grill
  • 3Potatoes in a pan of water → boil
  • 4Vegetables over coals → barbecue
  • 5Whole chicken in the oven → roast
  • 6Cakes in the oven → bake
Ex.3 in context: 1 barbecue lamb chops · 2 boil water for pasta · 3 bake a cake · 4 fry steak / grill it · 5 roast potatoes / boil them first.
Exam Task · multiple-choice cloze

Where did the hamburger come from?

  • 1A popular foods in the world
  • 2while they C rode their horses
  • 3they ate the meat D without cooking it
  • 4a type of B fried meat in bread
  • 5Otto A mixed meat with eggs
  • 6when the sailors B went back to America
Strategy: look at the word right before and after each gap first — gap 3's "cooking" (an -ing form) only follows "without" of the four options.
Grammar Ex.1 · past simple, concept check

Match the use

  • 1Rachel went, bought and took → b, actions one after the other
  • 2I prepared supper last night → c, a finished action
  • 3James had a sandwich every day → a, a past routine
Note: the same verb form does three different jobs — sequence, single finished event, or repeated habit; only the context tells you which.
Grammar Ex.2 · A giant pizza!

Complete with the past simple

  • 1Pizza started in Italy.
  • 2Chefs worked together for hours.
  • 3It was two kilometres long!
  • 4They used more than 2,200 kg of cheese.
  • 5The chefs ate some of it.
  • 6They also gave more than 20,000 pieces away.
All six verbs describe a completed record-breaking event, so the past simple runs through the whole text.
Grammar Ex.3 · past continuous, concept check

Match the use

  • 1Yasmin was watching while dad was cooking → b, two actions together
  • 2It was raining, the wind was blowing → d, background information
  • 3I was making breakfast at six → a, one action in progress
  • 4Lin was preparing lunch when the phone rang → c, interrupted by another action
REMEMBER: stative verbs never take the continuous — "I didn't understand", never "I wasn't understanding".
Grammar Ex.4 · build the sentence

Write it in the past continuous

  • 1What were you doing at one o'clock on Monday? / I was eating my lunch and listening to the radio.
  • 2This time last week we were grilling burgers in my aunt's garden.
  • 3Candice wasn't dancing at the party because she was serving food to the guests.
  • 4Maria and Alfred were arguing about the best pizza toppings for hours last night.
Every sentence needs "was/were + -ing"; watch subject-verb agreement (were for they/we, was for I/she).
Grammar Ex.5 · the real contrast

Simple or continuous?

  • 1We finished cleaning and then we ate ice cream.
  • 2Did Ludmila call you while you were watching the cookery show?
  • 3My mum cooked dinner because dad was ill.
  • 4Daisy and Tom were making biscuits when Marcus walked in.
  • 5I didn't fry the burgers; I grilled them.
  • 6Dee was tidying the kitchen while Pete was helping Dan.
Pattern: sequence or single finished fact → simple. Ongoing action interrupted, or two things happening together → continuous.
