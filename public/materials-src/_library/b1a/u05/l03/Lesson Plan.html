New Close-up B1 Split A, Unit 5 · Where we live · SB p.58-59
PET I · 90 min
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can identify a speaker's opinion in a listening text and complete a multiple-choice listening task, and can expand on a topic in a general conversation about homes and neighbourhoods.
Target language. Alternative housing (houseboat, tiny house, campervan, green-roof, portable, miniature); useful language for asking about and giving opinions on homes (my favourite room is..., I'd love to change..., the main problem with where I live is...).
Exam link. B1 Preliminary Listening Part 3 (multiple choice with one long conversation, p.58) and Speaking Part 3/4-style general conversation (p.59) are both direct exam tasks.
Materials: Student's Book pp.58-59. Audio Tracks 5.3, 5.4, 5.5. Answers_VISTA.html (listening + speaking exercises). Somerset Arcade: b1a-u05-l03. If the play from l02 didn't happen, it goes here, after speaking.

1–3. Listening: the Tiny House Movement · 30 min

Materials: Student's Book p.58, Ex.1–6. Audio Tracks 5.3, 5.4, 5.5.

Vocabulary: houseboat (a house that floats on the water), campervan (a large vehicle that several people can stay or live in), portable (something that can be moved easily), miniature (very small), green-roof (a roof that has plants growing on it), possessions (things we own).

Ex.3 (match answers to questions): 1d · 2a · 3e · 4b · 5c.
Ex.5 (which questions ask about opinion): Questions 1, 4 and 6.
Ex.6 (Exam Task): 1B (many people think possessions make us happy) · 2B (above the kitchen, in a sleeping loft) · 3C (to have enough space for her possessions) · 4B (she has more free time than most people) · 5B (doesn't spend a lot of money on electricity) · 6A (she likes going to different places with them, i.e. meeting at the library or a café).

Valencia link: ask if students have seen the traditional wooden barracas of the Albufera near Valencia, or any small eco-houses in the huerta of L'Horta — are these a Spanish version of the "tiny house" idea?

4–7. Speaking: describing your home · 40 min

Materials: Student's Book p.59, Ex.1–6.

Ex.2 items to classify: close to friends (A), a modern kitchen (A), close to a large park (A), near busy roads (D), good WiFi and mobile signal (A), a garden (A), good public transport (A), shops nearby (A), lots of pollution (D), a big bedroom (A) — students' own weighting in Ex.3, but the fixed advantage/disadvantage judgement is objective.

Ex.2: 1 A · 2 A · 3 A · 4 D · 5 A · 6 A · 7 A · 8 A · 9 D · 10 A.

Valencia link: ask students to compare living in the centre of Valencia (close to shops, good public transport, but busy and noisy) with living in a nearby town like Alboraia or Paterna (bigger homes and gardens, but a longer commute) — which set of advantages matters more to them?

8. Somerset Arcade / Play · 10 min

Materials: laptop + projector, unit b1a-u05-l03.

Point Grab reviews today's listening/speaking vocabulary. Carry the l02 play here if it didn't happen, after speaking, per the group's standard pattern.

Fast finisher

Fast finishers write three more advantages or disadvantages of a neighbourhood, not already listed in Ex.2, and classify them A/D.

If short of time, cut:

Speaking Ex.3 (ranking and comparing, block 4) can be shortened to a quick show of hands. Do not cut the Exam Task (block 3 or 7): both are the exact listening and speaking exam formats tested at the end of the unit.
