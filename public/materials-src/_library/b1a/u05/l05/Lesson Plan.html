New Close-up B1 Split A, Unit 5 · Where we live · SB p.62-64
PET I · 90 min
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can respond to news and write an appropriately-styled email using all the notes given, and can reflect on positive ways to deal with difficult situations.
Target language. Responding to news (apologies, explanations, expressing enthusiasm, asking for/giving information, thanking, accepting/declining, giving advice); formal vs informal register.
Exam link. B1 Preliminary Writing Part 1 (an email of about 100 words responding to all the notes) is the exact exam task on p.63, and is written IN CLASS today, per the group's standing rule.
Materials: Student's Book pp.62-64. Answers_VISTA.html (writing + Live well exercises). Paper/laptops for the writing task. Somerset Arcade: b1a-u05-l05.

1–3. Writing: responding to news · 45 min

Materials: Student's Book pp.62-63, Ex.1–7.
Ex.2 (match functions to notes): 1 respond to an invitation → c (Yes! ...) · 2 give your opinion → a (Say what I think about the countryside) · 3 make a suggestion → b (Why don't you...?) · 4 request more details → d (Ask Robbie...).
Ex.5: 1 yes, she does · 2 no, she doesn't · 3 yes, she does.
Ex.6 (match plan parts): 1e · 2d · 3c · 4a · 5b.
Ex.7 (Useful Language headings): 1 Making suggestions · 2 Accepting/Declining invitations · 3 Responding to news.

Valencia link: before writing, ask students to imagine their English-speaking friend has just moved to a Valencia neighbourhood like Ruzafa or Benimaclet — what would they say to reassure them, using the Useful Language?

4. Exam Task: writing an email · 35 min

Materials: Student's Book p.63 (the Lola email + four notes), paper or laptops.

Students write their ~100-word reply to Lola, responding to all four notes: asking whether Lola will get on with everyone at her new school and how to make new friends; giving advice ("If I were you, I'd..."); accepting the invitation to help decorate on Saturday; saying which evening plan they prefer (cinema vs staying in). Mark against the four B1 Preliminary writing criteria (content, communicative achievement, organisation, language) — keep the scripts for l06's error-review.

6. Live well, study well: difficult situations · p.64

Materials: Student's Book p.64.

This unit's life-skills page (odd units 1/3/5 get "Live well, study well" instead of a video). Two situations to discuss (a faulty computer game with no receipt; a friend returning a borrowed T-shirt dirty and stained) and the "Mind your Mind" tips on positive problem solving.

Ex.2 (match actions to responses): 1d · 2a · 3b · 4c.

Valencia link: ask students how they would calmly resolve a real everyday problem in Valencia, e.g. a mistake with a Mercado Central stallholder or a delayed EMT bus — using the Useful Language on p.64 (I understand that it's not your fault, but...).

Fast finisher

Fast finishers who finish the email early complete Project 1 on p.64 in writing: what they'd say to calmly respond to and offer a solution for a shop or friend problem.

If short of time, cut:

Live well, study well (block 6) can be reduced to a two-minute skim if the writing task runs long — never cut time from the writing task itself (block 4): it is graded and feeds l06's error-review.
