1. Unit 3 results deck · 20 min

Materials: the Unit 3 results PowerPoint (cloned .pptx, _method/level-ladder-results-scripts/), projector.

The Unit 3 test is the source data. Present the class score by section first, name what's solid and what's "not yet" per section, then say when each weak point gets a second pass this year. Individual cards stay anonymised on the shared screen; open a student's own card quietly on a laptop for a one-to-one moment, never project a name.

2–6. Reading: the sapeurs and sapeuses of Kinshasa · 60 min

Materials: Student's Book pp.41-43, Ex.1–6. Audio Track 4.1.

Full running order in the table above. The article describes a group of people in Kinshasa (Democratic Republic of the Congo) who dress in bright, expensive, well-made clothes despite having ordinary jobs and little money — sapeurs (men) and sapeuses (women). Word Focus: lining (the material on the inside of a jacket, skirt or dress), express (show how you think or feel), gather (come together), wealthy (rich).

Ex.3: C (they aren't rich, but sometimes they wear expensive clothes).
Ex.4–5 (Word Focus): 1 elegant → b, well-dressed · 2 stamp → c, put your foot down heavily · 3 tailor → a, a person who makes and repairs clothes.
Ex.6 (Exam Task): gap 1 → G (nobody knows how this tradition started) · gap 2 → F (then in the evening, they go home and dress up in the most elegant and well-made clothes) · gap 3 → C (when they go out together, people gather around to admire them) · gap 4 → A (so how can the sapeurs afford their amazing clothes?) · gap 5 → D (it may seem strange for people who aren't wealthy to spend so much money on their appearance). Options B, E and H are the three extras, not used.

Valencia link: ask students if Valencia has anything similar to a sapeur culture — the falleras and fallers who dress in traditional, expensive, hand-made regional costume for Fallas, often saving for months for the fabric and the silk. Would they call that "elegant" in the same sense as the article?
