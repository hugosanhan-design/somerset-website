New Close-up B1 Split A, Unit 4 · That's the fashion · SB p.44-45
PET I: Wednesday, 15:45–17:15
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can name common clothes, accessories and materials, use adjectives describing clothing style, and form defining and non-defining relative clauses accurately with who, which, whose, when and where.
Target language. Clothes/accessories (bracelet, button, coat, earrings, glasses, glove, handkerchief, pocket, ring, scarf, tie, watch); materials (wool, silk, leather, cotton, metal, glass); style adjectives (antique, casual, fashionable, loose, old-fashioned, original, smart, tight); relative pronouns who/which/that/whose/when/where, defining vs non-defining clauses.
Exam link. Relative clauses reappear in the open cloze task on p.49 and in the Unit 4 test's grammar section.
Materials: Student's Book pp.44-45. Answers_VISTA.html (reveal deck, all exercises from both pages). Somerset Arcade: b1a-u04-l02. Review play (if time): plays/Unit4_TheFashion_ClothesAndRelativeClauses_Play.html.

2–4. Vocabulary: clothes, adjectives & materials · 45 min

Materials: Student's Book p.44, Ex.1–6.

Ex.1: three photos of people wearing accessories — a woman in a sari (bracelet, ring, earrings, scarf), a man in a suit (button, glasses, handkerchief, pocket, tie, watch), a woman in winter clothes (coat, glove, scarf, button, pocket). Ex.2: match eight style adjectives to their meanings. Ex.4: choose wool/silk/leather/cotton/metal correctly in context. Ex.6: complete four shop conversations (getting dressed for cold weather; choosing an outfit for a party; a birthday gift of earrings; a grandfather's smart suit).

Ex.1: Photo a: bracelet, ring, earrings, (scarf). Photo b: button, glasses, handkerchief, pocket, tie, watch. Photo c: coat, glove, scarf, button, pocket.
Ex.2: 1 antique → e, old and usually valuable · 2 casual → d, suitable to wear in your free time · 3 fashionable → f, modern and stylish · 4 loose → g, not fitting closely to your body · 5 old-fashioned → a, not modern · 6 original → h, unusual and interesting · 7 smart → c, suitable to wear for work in an office · 8 tight → b, fitting closely to your body.
Ex.4: 1 wool · 2 silk · 3 leather, silk · 4 cotton · 5 metal, glass.
Ex.6: 1 coat · 2 wool · 3 gloves · 4 blouse · 5 leather · 6 earrings · 7 glass · 8 watch · 9 tie.

Valencia link: ask which materials the falleras' traditional Fallas dresses are made of (silk for the fabric, gold for the jewellery, sometimes wool for the underskirts) and whether students would call that costume "smart", "original" or "old-fashioned" — and why more than one adjective can be true at once.

5–7. Grammar: relative clauses · 30 min

Materials: Student's Book p.45, Ex.1–6.

Ex.1: "My grandmother, who lives in Italy, wears glasses" (non-defining, extra information, commas) vs "The woman who lives next door wears glasses" (defining, identifies who the sentence is about). Ex.3: choose whose/which/where/when/that correctly. Ex.4: combine two sentences into one with a non-defining clause, remembering the commas. Ex.6: complete a text about fast fashion and second-hand clothes.

Ex.1: 1a (non-defining) · 2b (defining).
Ex.2 (rules): 1 who (people) · 2 which, that (things) · 3 whose (possession) · 4 when (a period of time) · 5 where (a place).
Ex.3: 1 whose · 2 which · 3 where · 4 when · 5 that · 6 whose.
Ex.4: 1 John's jacket, which is red, has got silver buttons · 2 This tie, which is silk, cost $40 · 3 Yesterday I met my friend Tessa, who makes her own clothes · 4 Last Saturday we saw our teacher, who was buying a suit at the department store · 5 I've bought a new raincoat, which is black with yellow spots.
Ex.5: 1 A mobile phone is something which we use to call friends · 2 A cinema is a place where we watch films · 3 A zoo is a place where we see animals · 4 A teacher is a person who teaches people · 5 A backpack is a bag which we carry on our backs · 6 A raincoat is a coat which we wear when it rains.
Ex.6: 1 which · 2 who · 3 which · 4 who · 5 where · 6 which · 7 which.

Valencia link: board example for Ex.5's pattern — "The Mercado Central is a place where you can buy fresh produce" or "Ruzafa is a neighbourhood which has a lot of vintage clothes shops." Each student writes one sentence about a Valencia place using who/which/where.

Fast finisher

Fast finishers write three sentences describing a classmate's clothing using a relative clause in each ("Maya is wearing a jumper which is made of wool"), without saying the name aloud — the rest of the group guesses who.
