Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can organise a story into a beginning, middle and end using sequencing words, write a 100-word story in exam conditions, and extract facts and vocabulary from a short documentary video about plastic pollution.
Target language. Sequencing words (one day, suddenly, then, at first, but then); feelings/places/weather adjectives for descriptive writing; video vocabulary (fleece, microbeads, synthetic, contaminate, scrub, shed, float, plankton, bloodstream).
Exam link. B1 Preliminary Writing Part 2 (the 100-word story) is written for real today, individually, in class — never as homework.
Writing is always done in class, never homework. Students write the real Exam Task (about 100 words) individually under exam-like conditions and it is collected at the end for marking. The corrective slides, Arcade round and per-student Fix-It Sheets follow next class (l06), per the group's standard error-review sequence.
Materials: Student's Book pp.50-52. Video: "From fleece jackets to your food" (SB p.52; script via the Teacher's Book, for reference if the clip can't play). Answers_VISTA.html (writing exercises + video factsheet, both with keys; the Exam Task itself has no key, it's the students' own writing). Somerset Arcade: b1a-u04-l05 (built from the video and sequencing words, since this page teaches writing skills and video content, not a wordlist).

2–5. Writing: organising a story · 35 min

Materials: Student's Book pp.50-51, Ex.1–6.

Ex.1: Will's story (a mysterious map appears after a stranger drops it). Ex.3: "Emma and the bird" — a bird brings Emma a scarf and an earring with a mysterious note. Ex.5: complete sentences describing feelings, places and weather using adjectives from the Useful Language box (nervous/calm, tiny/bright, noisy/busy, excited/bored, old/dirty).

Ex.1: 1 One day · 2 Suddenly · 3 Then · 4 At first · 5 But then.
Ex.2: 1 No, the teacher has not given the title · 2 about 100 words.
Ex.3 (paragraph order): 1b · 2d · 3a · 4c.
Ex.4: 1T · 2F · 3F · 4T.
Ex.5: 1 relaxed · 2 warm · 3 busy/noisy · 4 excited · 5 old/dirty/clean.

Valencia link: the Exam Task's setting is open, so encourage students to place their story somewhere real: the Turia gardens at night, a market stall at the Mercado Central, or a Fallas bonfire crowd — a specific Valencia setting makes the beginning ("the place") stronger than an invented one.

6–7. Exam Task: writing a story · 30 min

Materials: exam-task box, p.51. Plain paper or a printed template.

Task: "Your English teacher has asked you to write a story. Your story must begin with this sentence: 'When I saw the jacket in the shop, I was amazed.' Write your story in about 100 words." Students plan first (Ex.7, using the Exam Tip on thinking of ideas: characters, setting, a clear beginning/middle/end), then write individually. Collect every script at the end for marking — this feeds directly into l06's writing error-review and the personalised Fix-It Sheets.

8. Video: From fleece jackets to your food · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.52. Video clip + factsheet.

Before you watch: discuss what effect rubbish has on the environment and whether students see any of these effects where they live. While you watch: tick which of six listed plastic-pollution sources (fleece jackets, plastic bags, toothpaste with microbeads, face wash with microbeads, balloons, synthetic clothing) are actually mentioned. After you watch: complete the summary with the word box (chemicals, clothes, contaminated, microplastics, scrub, tiny, toothpaste), check on a second viewing.

While you watch (Ex.2), mentioned: 1 fleece jackets · 3 toothpaste with microbeads · 4 face wash with microbeads · 6 synthetic clothing. (Not mentioned: 2 plastic bags, 5 balloons — though the photo shows a plastic bag, the narration itself doesn't name it.)
Ex.3 (definitions): 1 contaminate · 2 scrub · 3 shed · 4 with the naked eye · 5 float · 6 plankton · 7 bloodstream.
Ex.4 (summary): 1 clothes · 2 tiny · 3 microplastics · 4 chemicals · 5 contaminated · 6 toothpaste · 7 scrub.

Video script (from the Teacher's Book, for the teacher's reference if the clip can't play): "One thing I bet you didn't know is that every time you wash your fleece jacket, 2,000 pieces of plastic get shed right into the waterway. The plastics are so small you can't see them with your naked eye, they're called microplastics. These tiny little plastics, as they float their way down the rivers and into our oceans, they're picking up different chemicals; they act like little sponges, picking up DDT and PPA and pseudo oestrogens. That matters because the fish and other marine life that think that it's plankton will end up eating those plastics. Bigger fish eat those fish and eventually, the fish make their way onto our dinner tables. It's showing up in honey, it's showing up in different food products that should not be contaminated with plastics. What we've learned over the last few years is that this is getting into the water from washing your fleece jackets; it's washing your synthetic clothing in general; it's coming from the microbeads that are in your face washes, the little blue things that show up in your toothpaste, that we think of as these 'miracle scrubbers'! No! They're plastics, and you're putting plastics into your mouth, into your bloodstream, and then they make their way into our oceans and into the bloodstream of the planet."

9. Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 10 min

Materials: laptop + projector, unit b1a-u04-l05.

Built from the video's vocabulary and facts, and today's sequencing words, since this page teaches a writing skill and video content rather than a wordlist.

Fast finisher

Fast finishers who finish the story early write one extra sentence continuing the story past its ending, adding a further twist.

If short of time, cut:

The carry-over play (block 1) first. If the video runs long, skip the after-you-watch summary gap-fill and give students the answers directly — do not cut the writing task itself or shorten its 25 minutes; a rushed exam-condition script is worse than a slightly shorter video segment.
