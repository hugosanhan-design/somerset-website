Listening Ex.1 · word types

Match the type of word

  • 1approximately → c, close to a number
  • 2seal → b, a sea mammal
  • 3a shark's teeth → c, a large number
  • 4tail → a, a part of an animal's body
  • 5sense → d, one of the five senses
  • 6dolphin → e, an intelligent sea mammal
Knowing what TYPE of word fills a gap (a number, a country, a body part) before listening makes the actual listening much easier.
Listening Ex.2 · Great White Sharks

Complete the facts

  • 1Length: 4 to 5 metres (biggest ever recorded: 7 metres)
  • 2Colour: grey
  • 3Number of teeth: around 3,000
  • 4What they usually eat: seals or dolphins
  • 5Their powerful tail lets them swim fast
  • 6Their best sense: smell
Sharks can't see or hear especially well, but their sense of smell is extremely good — a useful fact for the Exam Task that follows about why sharks investigate objects in the water.

Listening Ex.4 · Exam Task, gap fill

Why don't sharks attack more often?

  • 1In 2019, there were 64 recorded shark attacks on people.
  • 2Most attacks happened in the US (with eleven in Australia).
  • 3Sharks want to discover information about objects in the sea.
  • 4Unlike seals, humans have got a lot of bones and not enough fat.
  • 5Humans kill around 100 million sharks every year.
  • 6Sharks are killed for sport, by mistake, or to make some expensive foods.

Speaking Ex.1 · the environmental-action poster

Complete the poster

  • 1Write to your local council about a problem.
  • 2Recycle plastic, paper and glass.
  • 3Plant a tree in your garden or local park.
  • 4Take public transport instead of the car.
  • 5Reduce how much water and energy you use.
  • 6walk or cycle for short journeys.
  • 7Talk to your friends and family about the environment.
  • 8clean up your local beach, park or street.
These eight actions feed directly into the Writing lesson (l05): the writing task is an email describing things you've done to protect the environment.
Speaking Ex.3 · listening to instructions

Complete the instructions

  • 1Talk together for about two minutes.
  • 2A school committee is deciding.
  • 3Which projects its students could do.
  • 4To help protect the environment.
  • 5Talk about the different things students could do.
  • 6Then say which project would be best.
Exam Tip: if you don't understand an instruction in the real exam, it's fine to ask the examiner to repeat it — "I'm sorry, could you repeat the question, please?"
Speaking Ex.4 · Exam Task, picture discussion

Talk together for two minutes

Open-ended speaking — no answer key. Project it and let pairs talk through the useful language below while they complete the task.

Useful Language
Giving an opinion: "I think the best idea is...", "In my opinion..."
Asking your partner: "What do you think about...?", "Do you agree?"
Agreeing / disagreeing: "I agree, because...", "I'm not sure, because...", "On the other hand..."
Deciding together: "Shall we decide on...?", "So, our final choice is..."
