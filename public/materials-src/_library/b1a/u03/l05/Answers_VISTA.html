Writing Ex.1 · lead-in

What are you going to write?

  • 1What are you going to write? → an email
  • 2Who are you writing to? → a friend
  • 3What is it about? → things you've done to protect the environment
  • 4Is the style formal or informal? → informal
Writing to a friend always means informal language: contractions, exclamation marks, short sentences.
Writing Ex.2-3 · the model email

True, false, and informal phrases

  • 1The writer has been very busy lately. → F
  • 2The writer stopped walking to school. → F (started walking to school)
  • 3The writer repaired their sister's old bag. → T
  • 4The local supermarket doesn't sell bottled water any more. → T
Informal expressions used: "Hi", "How are things?", "Write soon".
Writing Ex.4 · structure

Match the paragraph

  • 1Opening/greeting paragraph → d
  • 2Main news paragraph → a
  • 3Follow-up detail paragraph → b
  • 4Closing paragraph → c
Every informal email needs these four moves: greet, give the main news, add detail, close warmly.
Writing Ex.7 · planning notes

Underline the key points

an email … to a friend … about things you and your friends have done … look after the environment … describe the things you have done at home and … at school

Plan with short phrases like these, never full sentences — that saves time for the actual writing.
Writing Ex.8 · Exam Task

Write your email (100-130 words)

Open exam task, no answer key. Written individually, in class, then collected for marking before l06.

Useful Language
Greeting: "Hi [name], How are things?"
Giving news: "You won't believe what I did last week...", "Guess what?"
Describing actions: "I've started...", "We've stopped using...", "I've been recycling..."
Closing: "Write soon!", "Let me know what you think!", "Speak soon!"
Live well, study well p.40 · useful vocabulary

The environment, achieving change

  • 1break down (phr v) → when something becomes smaller and smaller until it disappears completely
  • 2end up (phr v) → arrive at a place in a way that wasn't planned
  • 3gas (n) → a type of air, sometimes harmful
  • 4journey (n) → a trip from one place to another
  • 5persuade (v) → get someone to do something by giving good reasons
Add "break down" and "end up" to your phrasal verb notebook — both reappear across the rest of the course.
