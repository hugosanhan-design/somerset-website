Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can name eight geographical features (stream, glacier, valley, cave, rainforest, coast, ocean, cliff) and use the present perfect simple and continuous correctly, including with for/since and other time expressions.
Target language. Geographical features vocabulary; present perfect simple vs continuous; have/has + past participle; have/has + been + -ing.
Exam link. Present perfect forms appear across every Reading, Use of English and Writing task this year; distinguishing it from the past simple (Unit 2) is one of the most common B1 error areas.

2. Vocabulary: geographical features · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.32, Ex.1–2.

Ex.1: cover the word box first, brainstorm features students already know, then match the eight words to their gaps. Ex.2: complete seven sentences using the same words in context.

Ex.1: 1 stream · 2 glacier · 3 valley · 4 cave · 5 rainforest · 6 coast · 7 ocean · 8 cliff.
Ex.2: 1 My aunt lives in a house on the coast and swims in the ocean every morning. · 2 There are 427 different types of mammals in the Amazon rainforest. · 3 There was a deep valley between the two mountains. · 4 We filled our bottles with water from the stream. · 5 Many tourists visit Antarctica to see its huge glaciers. · 6 Don't stand so close to the edge of the cliff! It's dangerous. · 7 In 1940, four boys discovered some amazing paintings in a cave in Lascaux, France.

Valencia link: the Albufera (a coastal lagoon, not far from a real ocean coast) and the Turia riverbed (once a real river, now a park after it was diverted following the 1957 flood) are both geographical features students already know locally — ask which of the eight words could describe each one.

3. Vocabulary: climate change collocations · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.32, Ex.3–4.

Match each word to its collocation partner, then use the resulting phrases in short sentences about protecting the environment.

Ex.3: 1 fossil → e fuel · 2 renewable → a energy · 3 solar → b power · 4 produce → c electricity · 5 heavily → d polluted.
Ex.4 (in context): we should stop burning fossil fuel and switch to renewable energy, such as solar power, to produce electricity without leaving rivers heavily polluted.

4–5. Grammar: present perfect simple · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.33, Ex.1–3.

Ex.1: complete the rule (present perfect = have/has + past participle). Ex.2: match three example sentences to their use — an unfinished action ("I've worked here for five years"), a finished action where we don't mention the time ("I've done a lot of work today"), and an experience ("I've never been to Japan"). Ex.3: complete three sentences with the present perfect simple.

Ex.1: a have · b past participle.
Ex.2: matches the three uses above (unfinished action; finished action, no time given; experience).
Ex.3: 1 She has had this job for six years. She travelled to Rome for training and went to a conference last month. · 2 James has just bought a new bike. · 3 Things have changed a lot since I studied at this school.

6–8. Grammar: present perfect continuous · 25 min

Materials: Student's Book p.33, Ex.4–7.

Ex.4: complete the rule (present perfect continuous = have/has + been + -ing). Ex.5: match three example sentences to their use. Ex.6: complete four sentences, noticing that item 4 uses the simple form because it states a specific quantity. Ex.7: complete the text about the International Ice Patrol, mixing past simple, present perfect simple and present perfect continuous.

Ex.4: a have · b been · c -ing form.
Ex.5 (uses): "I've been working here for five years" (an unfinished action, continuous), "She's just been telling him" (a recently finished action with an effect now), "I've been painting this room all day and I'm exhausted" (the action and effort, not just the result).
Ex.6: 1 The ice at the North Pole has become much thinner over the last 50 years. · 2 Climate change has caused the ice to melt faster every year. · 3 The weather has been getting warmer every year. · 4 Scientists have caught 50 kilos of plastic in the ocean this month.
Ex.7: In the past, icebergs were a serious danger to ships. In the last 200 years, hundreds of ships have sunk after they hit icebergs. In 1912, the Titanic hit an iceberg and over 1,500 people died. In 1914, the International Ice Patrol formed to track dangerous icebergs. Ever since then, the Ice Patrol has been giving ships warnings about ice, and it has probably saved thousands of lives.

Valencia link: ask students what has changed about Valencia's own coastline since the 1957 Turia flood led the river to be diverted away from the city centre — "The city has changed a lot since the river was moved" is a real local example of the same present perfect pattern as Ex.3.
