Vocabulary Ex.1 · geographical features

Name the feature

  • 1A small, narrow river → stream
  • 2A huge, slow-moving mass of ice → glacier
  • 3Low land between two mountains → valley
  • 4A large hole in the side of a mountain or under the ground → cave
  • 5A thick forest with very high rainfall → rainforest
  • 6The land next to the sea → coast
  • 7A huge body of salt water → ocean
  • 8A high, steep rock face, often at the coast → cliff
These eight words appear throughout the unit — the reading (coast, ocean) and the grammar text about the Titanic (icebergs at sea) both reuse them.
Vocabulary Ex.2 · in context

Complete the sentence

  • 1My aunt lives in a house on the coast and swims in the ocean every morning.
  • 2There are 427 different types of mammals in the Amazon rainforest.
  • 3There was a deep valley between the two mountains.
  • 4We filled our bottles with water from the stream.
  • 5Many tourists visit Antarctica to see its huge glaciers.
  • 6Don't stand so close to the edge of the cliff! It's dangerous.
  • 7In 1940, four boys discovered some amazing paintings in a cave in Lascaux, France.
Item 7 is a real event: the Lascaux cave paintings, discovered in France in 1940, are among the most famous prehistoric art in the world.

Grammar Ex.1-2 · present perfect simple

Complete the rule

  • 1We form the present perfect with have/has + the past participle of the verb.
  • 2"I've worked here for five years" → an unfinished action, still true now
  • 3"I've done a lot of work today" → a finished action, but we don't mention when
  • 4"I've never been to Japan" → an experience or life event, unfinished time (my life so far)
Practice sentences, not the book's wording: the quoted example sentences here are Somerset-written to illustrate the rule (the letter answers b/a/c/d match the printed key exactly); the SB's own example sentences could not be sourced (see BUILD_REPORT.md). Contrast with the past simple (Unit 2): "I worked here for five years, between 2000 and 2005" gives a finished time, so it takes the past simple instead.

Grammar Ex.4-5 · present perfect continuous

Complete the rule

  • 1We form the present perfect continuous with have/has + been + the -ing form of the verb.
  • 2"I've been working here for five years" → an unfinished action
  • 3"She's just been telling him" → a recently finished action with an effect now
  • 4"I've been painting this room all day and I'm exhausted" → the action and effort itself, not just the result
Compare "I've painted this room. I love the colour" (result, simple) with "I've been painting this room all day" (effort/duration, continuous).

  • 3The weather has been getting warmer every year.
  • 4Scientists have caught 50 kilos of plastic in the ocean this month.

Grammar Ex.7 · the International Ice Patrol

Past simple or present perfect?

  • 1In the past, icebergs were a serious danger to ships.
  • 2In the last 200 years, hundreds of ships have sunk after they
  • 3hit icebergs.
  • 4In 1912, the Titanic hit an iceberg and over 1,500 people died.
  • 5In 1914, the International Ice Patrol formed to track dangerous icebergs.
  • 6Ever since then, the Ice Patrol has been giving ships warnings about ice,
  • 7and it has probably saved thousands of lives.
Pattern: a specific finished date ("In 1912...") takes the past simple; an unfinished period ("Ever since then...") takes the present perfect.
