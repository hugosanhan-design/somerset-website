2. Vocabulary: environment verbs · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.36, Ex.1–2.

Ex.1: match break, destroy, prevent, reach, survive, set up, collect and remove to their definitions. Ex.2: complete a short text about the Great Pacific Garbage Patch (a real area of plastic rubbish in the North Pacific Ocean, kept together by ocean currents) using the same eight verbs.

Ex.2: Plastic in the ocean breaks into millions of tiny pieces called microplastics. This rubbish destroys habitats for fish and other sea animals. Some of it prevents turtles and other animals from finding food. Most of the rubbish never succeeds in reaching the shore, because it stays in the middle of the ocean. Many young sea creatures don't survive because they eat small pieces of plastic. A young inventor called Boyan Slat set up a project called The Ocean Cleanup to design machines that collect plastic. The machine collects plastic from the surface of the water using long floating barriers. Scientists hope The Ocean Cleanup can remove most of the patch's plastic within a few years.

Valencia link: ask students if they've seen local beach-clean days (e.g. at the Malvarrosa or El Saler) that collect and remove rubbish from the sand, the same verbs used for the ocean garbage patch on a much bigger scale.

3. Vocabulary: prepositions (1) · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.36, Ex.3.
Ex.3: 1 The eco club started before the summer holidays. · 2 We climbed over the fence to reach the abandoned garden. · 3 The plastic bottle came from a nearby factory. · 4 We put all the rubbish into three separate bins. · 5 Remember to add fresh water to the pond every week. · 6 There has been more rubbish in the river in the last decade. · 7 Someone threw an old tyre onto the grass. · 8 Look at the bacteria at the bottom of the pond.

4–5. Grammar: articles · 25 min

Materials: Student's Book p.37, Ex.1–2.

Ex.1: identify the countable and uncountable nouns in a set of sentences, then match each to a rule. Go through the rule table: a/an for a first mention or a job; the for something specific, unique nouns, oceans/rivers/mountain ranges, some countries, adjectives-as-nouns; zero article for proper names, sports/colours, days/months, most countries, general uncountable/plural nouns, and bed/school/hospital used for their purpose rather than the building.

Ex.1 rule table (examples): "I bought a new house. The house has got a garden." (a = first mention, the = specific) · "She's an engineer." (a/an = job) · "the Sahara", "the Nile" (unique geographical features) · "the poor", "the British" (adjective used as a noun) · "Sugar is bad for your health" vs "Pass me the sugar, please" (general vs specific) · "He's in hospital" (purpose) vs "The new hospital is well-equipped" (the actual building).
Ex.2: 1 an, The · 2 a · 3 – · 4 the, a · 5 The · 6 The · 7 The, the · 8 an, The · 9 an, the.

6. Grammar: Angel Falls · 20 min

Materials: Student's Book p.37, Ex.3.

Text about Angel Falls, the world's highest waterfall, in Venezuela. Complete all 16 gaps with a, an, the or – (no article).

Ex.3 (16 gaps): Angel Falls is (1)the highest waterfall in (2)– the world's most remote regions. It drops from (3)the top of (4)the mountain in (5)the south-east of Venezuela. (6)The waterfall gets its name from (7)an American pilot, Jimmie Angel, who saw it while flying over (8)the jungle in 1933. He tried to land his small plane near (9)the top of the falls, but the plane got stuck in the mud. Angel and his companions survived (10)the crash, but they had to walk for eleven days through (11)– thick jungle to reach (12)the outside world again. Today, visitors travel by canoe along (13)an unusual, winding river, through (14)– Canaima National Park, to get close to the falls, and from there they can see the falls from (15)a small aeroplane or from (16)the ground.

Note: this passage is a Somerset-written text built to carry the printed answer sequence (the original page could not be sourced), so treat the rule-fit at each individual gap as a teaching approximation rather than the publisher's own wording — replace with the real text if it turns up.

Valencia link: ask students to name Spain's own most famous waterfalls or natural landmarks and decide which articles those names take (e.g. "the Pyrenees", "– Montserrat", "the Ebro").
