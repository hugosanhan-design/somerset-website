Opener p.29 · the northern lights

What's happening in the photo?

  • 1What do you think is happening in this photo? → student's own answer — people are standing outside in the snow, looking up at the aurora borealis
  • 2Why do these colourful lights appear? → the sun blows solar winds towards the Earth, and that causes these colourful lights
  • 3Which countries can you see the northern lights from? → Sweden, Norway, Finland, Denmark (especially Greenland), Canada, Russia, the US and Iceland
Why: the lights are caused by electrically charged particles from the sun colliding with gases like nitrogen and oxygen in the Earth's atmosphere.
Reading Ex.4 · comprehension

Emma Camp and the coral reefs

  • 1Where did Emma learn about marine biology? → she learned about marine biology in the UK
  • 2How do coral reefs form? → polyps have skeletons made of limestone that join together to make coral reefs
  • 3What happens to a reef when the water gets too warm? → the algae leave the corals and the corals die
  • 4Why do coral reefs matter so much? → because coral reefs are home to 25% of all marine species, provide food for millions of people and protect the coast from storms
Where to find it: line 5 (Q1), line 9 (Q2), line 21 (Q3), lines 17-19 (Q4) of the original article.
Reading Ex.5 · when did it start?

When did Emma become interested in the sea?

  • 1when she was six years old
  • 2she was amazed to see a whole new world
  • 3she knew then that she wanted to learn more about sea life
A first encounter with the sea as a child is often the origin story behind a real scientific career — a useful model for the "when did you first..." speaking questions later this year.
