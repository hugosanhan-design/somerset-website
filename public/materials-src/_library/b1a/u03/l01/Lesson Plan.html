2. Opener p29 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.29.

Photo of the aurora borealis (Northern Lights) seen from the ground. In pairs, discuss what causes the lights and where they can be seen.

Answers: 1 students' own answers · 2 the sun blows solar winds towards the Earth, and that causes these colourful lights · 3 Sweden, Norway, Finland, Denmark (especially Greenland), Canada, Russia, the US and Iceland.

Valencia link: ask if anyone has seen the Milky Way clearly outside the city (e.g. inland towards the Serra Calderona or the Sierra de Espadán) — light pollution over Valencia city means you never see it from the centre, but the northern lights are visible even further from the poles than most people expect.

3–5. Reading: coral reefs & Emma Camp · 45 min

Materials: Student's Book pp.30-31, Ex.1–6.

The reading is about Emma Camp, a marine biologist and National Geographic explorer who studies coral reefs. Ex.1–2 are lead-in discussion (own answers). Ex.3: students skim the article and check their Ex.2 ideas against it. Ex.4 Word Focus: work out marine biologist, snorkelling, coral reef and form from context, then answer four comprehension questions. Ex.5: when did Emma become interested in marine life?

Ex.4 comprehension: 1 she learned about marine biology in the UK · 2 polyps have skeletons made of limestone that join together to make coral reefs · 3 the algae leave the corals and the corals die · 4 because coral reefs are home to 25% of all marine species, provide food for millions of people and protect the coast from storms.
Ex.5: when she was six years old; she was amazed to see a whole new world; she knew then that she wanted to learn more about sea life.

Exam Tip: skim the text first for general meaning, then read it again more carefully for the ideas in each question.

Ex.6 (Exam Task, multiple choice with one text): 1C · 2B · 3C · 4B · 5B.

Valencia link: Spain has its own coral-adjacent marine conservation story much closer to home — the Posidonia oceanica seagrass meadows around the Balearic coast and the Mediterranean more broadly are protected for the same reason coral reefs matter here (they shelter huge numbers of species and protect the coast). Ask students if they've ever snorkelled off the Costa Blanca or the Comunidad Valenciana coast.

6. Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 10 min

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit b1a-u03-l01.

Group of six: two trios or one shared round. Question bank covers the reading's key vocabulary and facts about Emma Camp and coral reefs.

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to write three sentences describing what they think it would be like to explore a coral reef, using at least one Word Focus item (marine biologist, snorkelling, coral reef, form) each.

If short of time, cut:

The individual results-deck student cards (block 1) — keep the class-wide chart, click through individual cards only if time allows. Do not cut the Exam Task (Ex.6): it is the graded exam-format practice for this lesson.
