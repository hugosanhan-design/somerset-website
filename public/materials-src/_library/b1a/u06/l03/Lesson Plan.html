2–4. Listening: the Patagonian Expedition Race · 35 min

Materials: Student's Book p.70, Ex.1–4. Audio tracks 6.3, 6.4.

Ex.1: cross out the option that doesn't fit in each of five sentences about an extreme race. Ex.3: before listening, predict what kind of word fits each of the six Exam Task gaps (place, number, type of weather, verb, animal, object). Ex.4: complete "The Four Deserts Race" fact file while listening.

Ex.1 (cross out): 1 limits doesn't fit ("the competitors/athletes must be extremely fit and strong") · 2 incredible doesn't fit ("one of the most challenging/toughest races") · 3 carry on doesn't fit ("competitors must deal with/cope with difficult conditions") · 4 windiest doesn't fit ("extremely hot/dry places") · 5 success doesn't fit ("only a small number of people can enter this event/competition").
Ex.3 (word type prediction): 1 place · 2 number · 3 type of weather · 4 verb · 5 animal · 6 object.
Ex.4 (Exam Task, The Four Deserts Race): Race starts in: 1 the Atacama Desert, in Chile. Length of each part: 2 250 km. Gobi Desert: strong 3 winds and snow in the mountains. Sahara Desert: difficult to 4 breathe because of sandstorms. Antarctica: penguins, seals and 5 whales; they sleep on real 6 beds.

Valencia link: compare the Four Deserts Race's toughness with the Valencia Marathon (Maratón Valencia Trinidad Alfonso) — both extreme tests of endurance, but one is a single city race and the other crosses four deserts over a year.

5–7. Speaking: giving opinions · 40 min

Materials: Student's Book p.71, Ex.1–6.

Ex.2: sort eight comments about sport (meeting people, needing equipment, having rules, being challenging, being cheap, being dangerous, being popular, being unusual) into advantages and disadvantages. Ex.6: the Exam Task itself has no single correct answer — assess the range and justification of opinions, not a fixed key.

Ex.2 (suggested answers): a A (helps you meet new people) · b D (needs special equipment) · c D (has got a lot of rules) · d D (is very challenging) · e A (isn't expensive) · f D (is quite dangerous) · g A (is very popular) · h A (is very unusual).
Ex.3–4: students' own ranking and pair comparison, no fixed answer.
Ex.6 (Exam Task): open picture discussion (hiking, football, gym workout, running, jet-skiing) for a busy friend who wants to get fit and meet people — assess using the Useful Language box (giving opinions, agreeing/disagreeing), not against a fixed key.

Valencia link: add a seventh "picture" to the discussion — padel in a Campanar club, popular, sociable, needs a partner and equipment — and ask where it would rank among the six official pictures.

8. Somerset Arcade · 10 min

Materials: laptop + projector, unit b1a-u06-l03.

Point Grab on listening vocabulary and the Useful Language for giving opinions/agreeing/disagreeing.

Fast finisher

Fast finishers write two more advantage/disadvantage comments (like Ex.2's list) about a sport of their choice and add them to the group's ranking discussion.

If short of time, cut:

Speaking Ex.3–4 (personal ranking/comparison) can be shortened to a quick show of hands if time is tight. Do not cut the Speaking Exam Task (Ex.6): it is the lesson's core exam-format practice.
