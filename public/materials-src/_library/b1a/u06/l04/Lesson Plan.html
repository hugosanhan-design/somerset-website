New Close-up B1 Split A, Unit 6 · Go for it! · SB p.72-73
PET I: Wed, 90 min
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can name sports clothes and verb+noun collocations for sport, build word families (compete, danger, depth, dive, fitness, freedom, profession, strength), and form and use second conditional sentences, including could instead of would.
Target language. Sports clothes (gloves, helmet, ice skates, swimming costume, tracksuit, trainers); collocations (do a workout, enter a competition, hit a ball, join a club, practise a skill, ride a horse, sail a boat, score a goal); word building (competitive, dangerous, deep, diver, fit, freedom, professional, strong); second conditional.
Exam link. The word-building text (Ex.5) and the second conditional gap-fills both recur, in a shorter form, in the Unit 6 test's Vocabulary and Grammar sections.
Materials: Student's Book pp.72-73. Answers_VISTA.html (reveal deck, all exercises from both pages). Somerset Arcade: b1a-u06-l04. Review play (if time): plays/Unit6_GoForIt_ClothesAndSecondConditional_Play.html.

2–4. Vocabulary: clothes, collocations & word building · 45 min

Materials: Student's Book p.72, Ex.1–5.

Ex.1: photos of a cyclist's helmet, a jogger's tracksuit, boxing gloves, trainers being laced, a swimming costume, ice skates. Ex.4: a noun/verb/adjective/adverb table (competition/competitor, danger, depth, diving/diver, fitness, freedom, profession/professional, strength). Ex.5: choose the correct option in a text about free diving.

Ex.1: 1 helmet · 2 tracksuit · 3 gloves · 4 trainers · 5 swimming costume · 6 ice skates.
Ex.3: 1 do → g, a workout · 2 enter → a, a competition · 3 hit → d, a ball · 4 join → f, a club · 5 practise → c, a skill · 6 ride → h, a horse · 7 sail → e, a boat · 8 score → b, a goal.
Ex.4 (word building table): 1 compete (verb) · 2 dangerous (adj) · 3 deep (adj) · 4 dive (verb) · 5 fit (adj) · 6 free (adj) · 7 professionally (adverb) · 8 strong (adj).
Ex.5 (free diving text): 1 competitive · 2 fit · 3 strong · 4 divers · 5 depth · 6 freedom · 7 professional · 8 dangerous.

Valencia link: ask which sports clothes from Ex.1 students would need for popular local activities — trainers and a tracksuit for the Turia Gardens running track, a swimming costume for the Malvarrosa, ice skates for the seasonal rink in the city centre at Christmas.

5–7. Grammar: second conditional · 30 min

Materials: Student's Book p.73, Ex.1–6.

Ex.1: second conditional uses past simple in the if-clause, would + infinitive in the main clause. Ex.2: use a) for imagined unlikely situations, use b) for advice. Ex.3: "could" can replace "would" to talk about ability. Ex.6 mixes first and second conditional in a football-club dialogue.

Ex.1: a past simple · b would.
Ex.2: 1 b (advice) · 2 a (imagined present/future).
Ex.3: could.
Ex.4: 1 lived · 2 would/could buy · 3 didn't enjoy · 4 practised · 5 would/could get · 6 wouldn't feel.
Ex.5: 1 If I had a bike, I wouldn't get the bus to the football club (example given) · 2 If he did some exercise, he would get fit/fitter · 3 If she was/were better at running, she would get chosen for teams · 4 If we had some money, we could buy new team uniforms · 5 If we had enough players, we wouldn't have to cancel the match · 6 If my friends had tickets, they could come into the stadium.
Ex.6: 1 were, wouldn't play · 2 will he win · 3 open, 'll/will see · 4 'll/will make · 5 would you do, would run · 6 were, would you play.

Valencia link: board example — "If I lived nearer the Turia Gardens, I would go running every day" — then each student writes one second conditional sentence about their own life in Valencia.

8. Somerset Arcade + Play · 10 min

Materials: laptop + projector, unit b1a-u06-l04. Play: groups/2026-27/pet1/plays/Unit6_GoForIt_ClothesAndSecondConditional_Play.html.

Point Grab covers today's sports clothes/collocations vocabulary and second conditional. If there's time, open the play (three scripts, each set in a real Valencia sport location); pick a 2- or 3-character version.

Fast finisher

Fast finishers write three second conditional sentences imagining they were a professional athlete in a sport of their choice, using at least one word-building form from Ex.4-5 in each.
