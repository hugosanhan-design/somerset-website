Aims. students can use modals of permission and obligation (can, could, mustn't, be not allowed to, ought to, be supposed to) accurately, distinguish be able to / be capable of / manage to / succeed in for ability, and use might/may/can't/must for present probability.
Target language. modals: could, can, mustn't, be not allowed to, ought to, be supposed to, may, be able to, be capable of, managed to, succeeded in, might, can't, must.
Exam link. B2 First Use of English Part 4 (key-word transformations) regularly tests exactly this modal group; also Reading and Listening comprehension of obligation/permission/probability.
60-minute session (FCE I). One dense grammar page, seven exercises. Friday B2 pairs this with l04 (Listening p.58 + Speaking p.59) in their 2-hour slot: run Blocks 1–5 below (about 50 min), break, then l04's plan.

1. Listening + rules: Exercises 1–2 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.57, Ex.1–2, Track 5.2.

Ex.1: listen to Shazad and Chloe talking about a drone. Ex.2: read six sentences from the conversation and complete rules a–f with the bold words (could, can, mustn't, be supposed to, be not allowed to, ought to).

Ex.1 answers: 1 The drone belongs to Rebecca (Chloe's sister). 2 Shazad flies it too far away and loses control of it so it crashes.
Ex.2 answers: a mustn't · b be not allowed to · c ought to · d be supposed to · e could · f can.

2. Personalise: Exercise 3 · 7 min

Materials: Student's Book p.57, Ex.3 (pairs discuss three prompts about the English lesson).

Open discussion, no fixed answer: one thing you can/aren't allowed to do in the English lesson; one thing you ought to do soon and one you mustn't do; one thing you're supposed to do soon, but don't want to.

Valencia link: add a fourth prompt: one thing tourists in Valencia are not allowed to do (e.g. swim in the Turia Gardens fountains) and one thing they ought to do (try horchata at a real horchateria in Alboraya).

3. Ability: Exercises 4–5 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.57, Ex.4–5.
Ex.4 answers: 1c (managed to → past achievement) · 2a (capable of → general ability, maximum limit) · 3b (be able to → future ability).

Ex.5: pairs choose the correct option (able/capable, succeeded/managed, capable/able) in three personalised questions and discuss them.

4. Probability: Exercise 6 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.57, Ex.6 (four extracts from the conversation).
Answers: the speaker sounds sure in extracts 3 (can't) and 4 (must). Extract 2 (may) sounds more formal than extract 1 (might).

5. Transformation: Exercise 7 · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.57, Ex.7 (rewrite eight sentences using the words in brackets).
Answers:
1 My phone must be in the house somewhere.
2 This computer is only a week old, so it can't be broken already.
3 Lisa is capable of performing amazing calculations in her head.
4 I don't understand how I'm supposed to install this software.
5 We finally managed to fix the computer after some difficulty.
6 My test results can't be right.
7 You ought to check the engine.
8 I'm sorry, but you're not allowed to use your phone in here.

Fast finisher

Write three sentences about your own devices at home: one with mustn't/ought to, one with capable of/managed to, one with a perfect-probability modal (must/can't + verb).

If short of time, cut:

Exercise 3's personalised discussion can be shortened to two prompts if the listening (Block 1) ran long. Do not cut Ex.7: the transformation practice is the exact B2 First Use of English task format for this grammar point.
