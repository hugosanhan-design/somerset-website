2. Lead-in: Exercise 1 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.54, Ex.1.

Small groups brainstorm ways technology keeps us safe, using the prompts given (accidents, crime, diseases, the planet) plus their own ideas. Open discussion, no fixed answer.

Valencia link: ask the group whether they know of any local safety technology — for example the Turia riverbed flood-control system built after the 1957 flood, which is exactly the kind of "protecting us from harm" the unit opener photo (Mars Desert Research Station) is asking about, on a very different scale.

3. Process verbs: Exercise 2 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.54, Ex.2 (match sentence beginnings 1–6 with endings a–f).
Answers: 1d (affect → you change it in some way) · 2f (cause → you make it happen) · 3a (examine → you study it carefully) · 4c (identify → you find out what it is) · 5b (measure → you check how big/hot/far it is) · 6e (monitor → you check it regularly).

4. First read: Exercises 3–4 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.54 (Exam Tip) and p.55, "How technology is keeping us safe" (parts 1–2).

Ex.3: quickly read the article and match the two headings (a: the surprising ways drones are saving lives; b: why NASA plans to slam a spacecraft into an asteroid) to parts 1–2. Ex.4: read the Exam Tip on coping with technical vocabulary, then look through each part again to find examples of technical vocabulary.

Ex.3 answers: a → part 2 (drones); b → part 1 (asteroid/DART).
Ex.4 (suggested answers): Part 1: asteroid, redirection, spacecraft, planetary. Part 2: drone, assignments.

Valencia link: the Exam Tip says a rough guess is often enough for technical words — ask the group to guess "asteroid" and "spacecraft" from context before checking, the same skill they need for any Valencia-set B2 First technical text (medicine, engineering, environment).

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to write one more true sentence for each of the six process verbs (affect, cause, examine, identify, measure, monitor), about something in their own daily life.

If short of time, cut:

Exercise 1's brainstorm (Block 2) can be shortened to two prompts rather than all four if the opener discussion ran long. Do not cut the Exam Tip (Block 4): coping with technical vocabulary is the whole basis for l02's exam task.
