Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can recognise the specific mistakes that came up in their own class's Unit 5 essay (mobile phone use), correct them, and apply the same correction to a new sentence of their own.

The six categories for this unit

Chosen from what Unit 5 actually teaches. Replace the bracketed description with the real, anonymised pattern once l07's scripts are marked.

  1. Essay register & topic sentences — too essay-like/impersonal opener, no rhetorical question, or a paragraph that doesn't open with a clear topic sentence (p.62 Learning Focus). A natural topic sentence some students reach for is their own mobile-phone habits at school or on the bus in Valencia — a fine hook, as long as it's followed through with a real argument.
  2. Modals of permission/obligation/ability mix-ups — confusing can/could/mustn't/be not allowed to, or able to/capable of/managed to/succeeded in (p.57).
  3. Perfect modals — wrong form after should/might/could/can't/must + have, or have + past simple instead of have + past participle (p.60).
  4. Task coverage — the essay must cover both the given viewpoint and the student's own opinion with supporting reasons; a one-sided or underdeveloped essay loses Content marks.
  5. Word count — outside the 140–190 word target.
  6. Spelling & linking words — British spelling, and correct use of contrast/result/example/addition linkers from the Useful Language box (p.63).

If short of time, cut:

Compress the fix-it sheet to homework rather than class time (Block 4) if the corrective-slide discussion (Block 2) runs long — but always leave time to physically hand out the sheet and explain it. Never cut Block 2 itself.
