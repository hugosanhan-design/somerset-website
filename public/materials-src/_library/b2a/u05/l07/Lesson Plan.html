New Close-up B2 Split A, Unit 5 · Emerging technologies
FCE I · Friday B2
Aims. students can plan and write a balanced opinion essay (140-190 words) about a modern invention or technology issue, using topic sentences, supporting reasons/examples, and a range of linking words for contrast, result, examples and adding points.
Target language. linking words: however/despite/in spite of (contrast), as a result/consequently/because of (result), for example/for instance (examples), in addition/furthermore/moreover/what's more (adding points), some people say/it is said that (reporting opinion).
Exam link. B2 First Writing Part 1 (compulsory essay). Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation and Language are the four marking criteria; this lesson targets Organisation directly.
60-minute session (FCE I). Writing is always done in class, never as homework. Friday B2 runs this as its own full lesson (not paired), given the length of planning + writing.
Materials: Student's Book pp.62-63. Answers_VISTA.html. Somerset Arcade: b2a-u05-l07. Scripts collected for l09.

1. Learning Focus + planning: Exercise 1 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.62, Learning Focus box + Ex.1.
Ex.1 answers: 1d (underline key words → to fully understand the topic and task) · 2a (plan the paragraphs → to stay on topic later) · 3e (plan vocabulary/grammar → to demonstrate how good your English is) · 4c (rephrase the task in the introduction → to avoid repeating the input wording) · 5b (include all the notes → to answer the question fully).

2. Analyse the task: Exercises 2–3 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.62, Ex.2–3 (example task: most important modern invention).
Ex.2 (suggested answers): 1 write an essay about an important modern invention. 2 two aspects. 3 how it has affected our lives, and any negative effects.

Ex.3: pairs discuss the most important modern invention, their main arguments for it, its negative aspects, and supporting examples — personal opinion, no fixed answer.

3. Model essay: Exercises 4–6 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.62, Ex.4 (model essay on television) and p.63, Ex.5–6.
Ex.5 (suggested answers): Paragraph 2: television is central to modern culture — entertainment, education, sports, news; available at home or on the phone; huge variety through streaming. Paragraph 3: one major drawback — some people spend too much time watching it, at the cost of study, exercise and socialising.
Ex.6 (which linking words do which job): 1 express contrast → However, despite, in spite of. 2 express result → Consequently, because of. 3 give examples → for instance. 4 add more points → What's more, In addition.

4–6. Plan and write the real Exam Task · 30 min

Materials: Student's Book p.63, Exam Task (mobile phone use limited to an hour a day — write an essay giving your opinion), Useful Language box, Reflection Checklist.

Students plan their own two main paragraphs and language choices (Ex.7), then write the real 140-190 word essay individually, in class, using the Useful Language for contrast/result/examples/adding points/reporting opinions/opposing views. Before finishing, they complete the Reflection Checklist (planned first, added supporting ideas, gave reasons/examples, used linkers, checked for mistakes).

Valencia link: suggest students who are short of an example use a real local one — e.g. schools in the Comunitat Valenciana that already restrict phone use during the school day — as supporting evidence for either side of the argument.

Fast finisher

Fast finishers who finish the essay early proofread a partner's script against the Reflection Checklist before handing both in.

If short of time, cut:

If time is very short, Ex.3's open pair discussion (Block 2) can be skipped and folded into the planning stage instead. Never cut the writing itself or set it as homework: house rule, and l09's error-review depends on today's real scripts.
