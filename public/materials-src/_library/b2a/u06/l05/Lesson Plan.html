Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can form question tags, turn indirect questions into normal questions and vice versa, and use negative questions to show criticism, surprise, sympathy or doubt.
Target language. Question tags (isn't it?, shall we?); indirect questions (word order, if/whether); negative questions (Weren't you...? Can't I...?).
Exam link. Question tags and indirect questions are a recurring Use of English (Part 4, key word transformation) source at B2 First.
Materials: Student's Book p.72. Answers_VISTA.html. Somerset Arcade: b2a-u06-l05. Audio: Track 6.8. Grammar reference 6.2-6.4, p.92.

1. Question tags: Exercises 1-3 · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.72, Ex.1-3.
Ex.1 answers: 1c 2e 3a 4b 5d.
Ex.2: you can add a question tag to the END of a sentence to invite another person to speak.
Ex.3 answers: 1 isn't it · 2 aren't I · 3 weren't there · 4 will you · 5 hadn't they.

Valencia link: practise with a true sentence about Valencia, e.g. "The beach gets very busy in summer, doesn't it?"

2. Indirect questions: Exercises 4-6 · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.72, Ex.4-6.
Ex.4 answers: 2 When does the film start? · 3 How much does a new printer cost? · 4 Did I lock the car? · 5 Have we met before?
Ex.5: to be more polite.
Ex.6 answers: a statement · b beginning.

3. Negative questions: Exercises 7-9 · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.72, Ex.7-9. Audio: Track 6.8.
Ex.7 answers: 1 weren't · 2 Didn't · 3 Isn't · 4 Hasn't · 5 Can't.
Ex.8: ...or when we expect our listener to AGREE with us.
Ex.9 answers (speaker 1-5 matched to function a-e): Speaker 1 → d (showing criticism) · Speaker 2 → a (expressing doubt or confusion) · Speaker 3 → c (showing surprise — "Wow! Isn't that runner fast?") · Speaker 4 → e (inviting the listener to agree) · Speaker 5 → b (expressing sympathy). Full key: 1d 2a 3c 4e 5b.

4. Escape room dialogue: Exercise 10 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.72, Ex.10.
Ex.10 answers: 1 are we · 2 Weren't · 3 what the code is · 4 Haven't · 5 there might · 6 there's · 7 can't I · 8 there · 9 shall.

Valencia link: Valencia has several real escape rooms (e.g. around Ruzafa and the old town) — ask if anyone in the group has tried one, and what clue types they remember.

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to write one negative question of their own for each function in Ex.9 (doubt, sympathy, surprise, criticism, inviting agreement) about a sport or hobby.

If short of time, cut:

The escape-room dialogue (Ex.10, Block 4) can be set as homework reading with answers checked next class, if Blocks 1-3 ran long. Do not cut Ex.9's listening (functions of negative questions): it is the piece students most often get wrong in speaking.
