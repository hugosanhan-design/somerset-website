Exercise 1 · match to the correct tag

Question tags

  • 1That's a great idea, → isn't it?
  • 2They don't need to pay, → do they?
  • 3There's never been any problem, → has there?
  • 4I'm talking too much, → aren't I?
  • 5Let's go home, → shall we?
Full key: 1c 2e 3a 4b 5d. Rule (Ex.2): you can add a question tag to the END of a sentence to invite another person to speak.

Exercise 4 · rewrite as normal questions

Indirect → direct

  • 1I'd like to know when the party starts. → When does the party start?
  • 2I'd like to know when the film starts. → When does the film start?
  • 3I wonder how much a new printer costs. → How much does a new printer cost?
  • 4Do you remember whether I locked the car? → Did I lock the car?
  • 5I'm not sure if we've met before. → Have we met before?
Why we use indirect questions (Ex.5): to be more polite. Rule (Ex.6): a) same word order as a STATEMENT; b) if/whether goes at the BEGINNING of the indirect question.
Exercise 7 · complete with negative question words

Negative questions

  • 1Why weren't you at practice yesterday?
  • 2Didn't you go hiking last week?
  • 3Wow! Isn't that runner fast?
  • 4Hasn't the weather been nice lately?
  • 5Can't you swim?
Rule (Ex.8): we use negative questions to show criticism, surprise, sympathy or doubt, or when we expect our listener to AGREE with us.
Exercise 9 · Track 6.8 · match speaker to function

What is each speaker doing?

  • Speaker 1(Why weren't you at practice?) → d — showing criticism
  • Speaker 2(Didn't you go hiking?) → a — expressing doubt or confusion
  • Speaker 3(Wow! Isn't that runner fast?) → c — showing surprise
  • Speaker 4(Hasn't the weather been nice?) → e — inviting the listener to agree
  • Speaker 5(Can't you swim?) → b — expressing sympathy
Full key: 1d 2a 3c 4e 5b.
