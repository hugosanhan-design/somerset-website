Exam Task · sentences A–G into gaps 1–6

Mira Rai: missing sentences

  • 1After "...often on an empty stomach." → D (she didn't know it, but this was great training for her future trail-racing career)
  • 2After "she had to leave school...difficult to afford." → G (so she began selling rice at the market, as a full-time job)
  • 3After "hadn't trained for such a distance." → B (to her surprise, she became the fastest woman in the race)
  • 4Before "Her injury meant she was unable to compete..." → F (while recovering from knee surgery, she helped two teenage runners from a remote village)
  • 5Before "The British ultra-runner, Lizzy Hawker, says..." → A (it's because of this that she wants to make an impact)
  • 6Before "In one scene, small children try putting on Rai's medals" → E (it may have already helped motivate some girls to think beyond their village lives)
Almost-right trap: sentence C ("When that happens, she meets a lot of young people who ask her how they can live their lives differently") is the unused extra — it sounds plausible near gap 4 or 6, but neither gap's surrounding sentence actually sets up "when that happens", so it doesn't fit either. Read the whole sentence before and after each gap, not just the keyword.
Vocabulary Exercise 1 · choose the correct word

Near-synonyms: success and fame

  • 1aRich had to overcome a lot of difficulties before he became successful.
  • 1bLisa couldn't cope with becoming famous so quickly.
  • 2aThere's a rumour that the band might split up.
  • 2bI love reading celebrity gossip.
  • 3aSome footballers were involved in a scandal that involved taking money.
  • 3bThe way they were treated by the media was a total disgrace.
  • 4aScientists have made a breakthrough in finding a cure.
  • 4bMy mum's favourite band are making a comeback.
Note the pattern: overcome/cope both mean dealing with difficulty, but "cope with" needs "with"; a breakthrough is a new discovery, a comeback is a return to former fame.
Vocabulary Exercise 2 · idioms

Fixed success expressions

  • 1Follow in her footsteps (have the same career).
  • 2Came out of nowhere (suddenly became famous).
  • 3Seized the chance (took the opportunity).
  • 4The secret of my success.
  • 5Open doors for you.
  • 6My only claim to fame.
  • 7Never got anywhere (no progress).
  • 8A household name.
All fixed phrases: these idioms don't work with other words substituted — learn them as whole chunks, not word by word.
