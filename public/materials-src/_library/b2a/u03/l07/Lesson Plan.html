Lesson Plan · b2a-u03-l07
New Close-up B2 Split A, Unit 3 · Role models · SB p.38-39
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can plan and write a B2 First story using the conventions of narrative fiction (clear beginning/middle/end, a range of past tenses, dramatic and concluding phrases), and have written and handed in a real, individually written story for marking.
Target language. Time/dramatic/concluding phrases for stories: out of the blue, in his/her wildest dreams, was absolutely stunned, at long last, to make matters worse, and the Useful Language box (It all began..., Suddenly..., Finally...).
Exam link. Writing Part 2, story option. Writing is always done IN CLASS, never as homework (house rule). The real Exam Task here is collected and marked, then returned in l09's error-review lesson.
Materials: Student's Book pp.38-39. Answers_VISTA.html (Learning Focus, Ex.6-7 key, planning grid). Somerset Arcade: b2a-u03-l07 (structure/language recap, not real student writing).

1–2. Lead-in and Learning Focus · 14 min

Materials: Student's Book p.38, the sample "crowd of fans" story.

The sample story is good at building tension (crowd getting bigger, narrator running) but weak on the ending — "Then, I woke up. It had all been a bad dream" is a cliché that many exam markers penalise. Go through the Learning Focus box: clear beginning/middle/end, don't add silliness that breaks the tone, don't just tell events in order — use past tenses to show what happened before.

3. Mark's story: Ex.4–6 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.38, Mark's story (missing ending).
Best ending: Ending 3 (Mark becomes a TV star; Musky is forgotten within a year) — it has a surprise, a clear end, and a positive outcome for Mark. Ending 1 is clear but boring/negative. Ending 2 isn't really an ending. Ending 4 (spacecraft, laser beam) is too silly and disconnected from the story's tone.

4. Language and planning: Ex.7–8 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.39, phrase bank + planning grid.
Ex.7 answers: 1 out of the blue · 2 in his wildest dreams · 3 was absolutely stunned · 4 At long last · 5 To make matters worse.

Students then write their own 4-paragraph plan (P1: big event, set the scene; P2: main events + background; P3: add drama/a problem; P4: clear ending) for the real Exam Task below.

5. Exercise 9: the real Exam Task, written in class · 23 min

Materials: Student's Book p.39, Exam Task box. Paper or laptops for the real, individually written story.

Exam Task (write in 140-190 words): Your teacher has asked you to enter a story writing competition for an English-speaking magazine. Your story must begin with this sentence: "I had never felt so nervous before in my life." Your story must include: a celebrity and a mistake.

Students write individually and in silence, using their plan and the Useful Language (time phrases, dramatic phrases, concluding phrases). Circulate but don't correct as they write. Collect every script at the end — these are marked before l09 (the writing error-review lesson).

Valencia link: before writing, mention as inspiration (not to copy) a real local example: a Valencian fan meeting a famous Valencia CF player or a well-known Fallera artist at a public event — a good jumping-off point for "a celebrity" in their own story.

6. Reflection Checklist · 3 min

Students tick the five checklist items (planned before writing; clear beginning/middle/end covering both prompts; added drama; used a range of past tenses; checked their work) before handing in.

Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 2 min

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit b2a-u03-l07.

Quick recap of story structure and the Useful Language phrases — not based on the real essays (those are analysed anonymously in l09, after marking).

If short of time, cut:

Ex.1's opening discussion (Block 1) can be shortened. Never cut the writing time itself (Block 5) — the house rule is that writing is always done in class, so there is no way to "finish it at home" if time runs short; extend into the following lesson's start instead if truly necessary.
