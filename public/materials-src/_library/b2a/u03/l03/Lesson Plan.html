New Close-up B2 Split A, Unit 3 · Role models · SB p.33
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can form and choose between the past perfect simple and the past perfect continuous, and can use them correctly to talk about actions completed before, or in progress up to, a point in the past.
Target language. Past perfect simple (had + past participle) vs past perfect continuous (had been + -ing).
Exam link. Grammar accuracy across Reading, Writing and Use of English; grammar reference at the back of the book (3.1, p.86).
Materials: Student's Book p.33 (+ grammar reference p.86). Answers_VISTA.html (full 4-exercise key). Somerset Arcade: b2a-u03-l03.

2–3. Exercises 1–2 + the rules · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.33.

Students decide whether sentences 1–4 use PPS or PPC, then answer questions a–d (which sentences describe a single completed action; how long a situation lasted; a process with an immediate result; the result of something completed some time ago).

Ex.1: 1 PPS · 2 PPC · 3 PPS · 4 PPC.
Ex.2: a 1, 3 · b 2 · c 4 · d 1.

Grammar guide: past perfect SIMPLE = actions/situations finished before another past action, OR how many actions had been completed by a point in the past ("Lucas had won several competitions before he was 16"). Past perfect CONTINUOUS = actions in progress up to a point in the past, often with an immediate result ("Mia had been baking cakes, so the house smelled delicious") or emphasising duration ("Anna had been writing songs for many years before her talent was recognised").

4. Exercise 3: the celebrity chef story · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.33, gap-fill conversation.

Students choose the correct option in each pair (simple vs continuous) in the story about meeting a TV chef in the park.

Answers: 1 I'd been sitting · 2 been running · 3 left · 4 run out · 5 asked · 6 been looking · 7 gone · 8 been waiting.

5. Exercise 4: Valentina Tereshkova · 18 min

Materials: Student's Book p.33, text about the first woman in space, word bank (become, do, finish, not tell, skydive, train, travel, volunteer, watch).

Students complete the text with the past perfect simple or continuous form of the verbs given.

Answers: 1 had become · 2 had volunteered · 3 had been skydiving · 4 had done · 5 hadn't told · 6 had been training · 7 had watched / had been watching · 8 had travelled · 9 had finished.

Valencia link: ask whether students know that Spain's own first (and so far only) astronaut, Pedro Duque, trained for years before his 1998 Space Shuttle mission — a good extra example sentence: "By 1998, Pedro Duque had been training as an astronaut for over a decade."

Fast finisher

Write two sentences about your own life: one using the past perfect simple (a completed action before another) and one using the past perfect continuous (something you'd been doing for a while).

If short of time, cut:

The final "your ideas" discussion (Block 6) can be dropped. Do not cut Exercise 4 (Block 5) — it's the fullest, most exam-realistic practice of both forms together.
