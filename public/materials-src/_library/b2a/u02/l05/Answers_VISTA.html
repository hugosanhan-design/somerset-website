Exercise 2 · complete the rules

Past simple or present perfect?

  • aUnfinished period (e.g. today, my life so far) → present perfect. Specific time (e.g. the first day) → past simple.
  • bFinished situations → past simple only. Started in the past, continues now → present perfect.
  • cFocus on the result now → present perfect. Focus on the time in the past → past simple.
Same underlying idea as Unit 1's present perfect page (l05 of Unit 1) — result/ongoing vs finished/specific time — now contrasted directly with past simple.

Exercise 4 · be used to / get used to

Match the meaning

  • 1"I'm gradually getting used to speaking Spanish." → c — it's becoming more and more normal
  • 2"I'm not used to eating a big meal at that time." → a — it doesn't feel normal yet
  • 3"Hopefully, you'll get used to it soon." → b — I hope it feels normal soon
Three-way trap: used to + infinitive (past habit, now stopped) vs be used to + -ing (a current state: familiar) vs get used to + -ing (a process: becoming familiar). All look alike; all mean different things.
Exercise 5 · gapped story

Ice swimming in Copenhagen

  • 1It's been a long winter so far.
  • 2Most people have been looking forward to spring for months.
  • 3Some of them have been swimming since they were very small.
  • 4…so they're used to the extreme cold by now.
  • 5Ice swimming has been part of Danish culture for a long time.
  • 6…you have to get used to the cold.
  • 7Carl (aged 12) has just emerged from the icy water.
  • 8Most members have hardly missed a day of school through illness.
  • 9"You'll get used to it after a few seconds," they tell me.
  • 10"How long did I last in the water?" I ask.
Valencia link: ask the group what visitors to Valencia typically have to "get used to" — a strong local parallel is eating dinner at 9–10pm, much later than in most other countries.
