Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can choose accurately between past simple and present perfect (a second contrast, alongside Unit 2's past simple/continuous), and can use be used to and get used to correctly — and distinguish them from used to + infinitive (l03).
Target language. Past simple vs present perfect (finished time vs unfinished period; results vs specific past time); be used to / get used to + -ing.
Exam link. This tense contrast and the used to family recur constantly in B2 First Use of English Part 4 transformations — today's page is effectively exam-transformation training in disguise.
60-minute session (FCE I). The used to/be used to/get used to three-way confusion is real — don't rush past Ex.4. Friday B2 pairs this with l06 (Use your English p.25) in their 2-hour slot: run Blocks 1–5 below (about 45 min), break, then l06's plan.

1–2. Listening lead-in + concept check · 18 min

Materials: Track 2.5. Student's Book p.24, Ex.1–2.

Ex.1 answers (suggested, from the key): what Harry enjoys — the people are kind and friendly; it's a great way to learn Spanish; his language course was excellent; it's very beautiful. What's difficult — communicating in Spanish; mealtimes are different, so he gets hungry. Ex.2: read three examples, choose which option (past simple/present perfect) fits each rule.

Ex.2 answers: a present perfect, past simple · b past simple, present perfect · c present perfect, past simple.

3. Controlled practice: Exercise 3 · 12 min

Materials: Student's Book p.24, Ex.3 (six sentences).
Answers: 1 has never performed, were · 2 was, didn't have, gave up · 3 haven't played, was · 4 wrote, has now become, has sold · 5 overslept, missed, was · 6 has started, hasn't finished.

4. be used to / get used to: Exercise 4 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.24, Ex.4 (three sentences, match to meanings a–c).
Answers: 1 "getting used to speaking Spanish" → c (it's becoming more and more normal) · 2 "not used to eating a big meal" → a (doesn't feel normal yet) · 3 "you'll get used to it soon" → b (I hope it feels normal soon).

Draw the three-way board contrast clearly: used to + infinitive (a past habit, now stopped) vs be used to + -ing (familiar with something, a state) vs get used to + -ing (becoming familiar, a process). All three look similar but mean different things.

5. Gapped story: Exercise 5 · 12 min

Materials: Student's Book p.24, "ice swimming in Copenhagen" article (ten gaps).
Answers: 1 been · 2 for · 3 were · 4 to · 5 has · 6 get · 7 has · 8 have · 9 used · 10 did.

Valencia link: ask the group: is there anything in Valencia people have to "get used to" the way the Danish ice-swimmers get used to the cold — e.g. tourists getting used to eating dinner at 9-10pm, much later than in most other countries?

Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 8 min

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit b2a-u02-l05.

Fast finisher

Write one sentence each with used to, be used to and get used to about your own life, then read them to the group — can they tell which is which?

If short of time, cut:

Ex.6 (the closing speaking prompt) is the first thing to drop if time is short — it's a nice-to-have, not exam-marked. Do not cut Ex.4: the three-way used to family confusion is exactly what B2 First transformations test.
