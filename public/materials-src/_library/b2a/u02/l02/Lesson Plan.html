2. Finish the exam task: Exercises 6–7 · 22 min

Materials: Student's Book pp.18-19, Exam Task (10 questions).

Students finish identifying sections A–D for each question, then commit to final answers. Reveal on Answers_VISTA.html. Go through at least 2–3 "almost right" distractors together — e.g. Q5 could tempt students toward section A (pirates/wax) as well as D (funeral payment); the specific detail ("proper funeral") is what decides it.

Answers: 1 A · 2 D · 3 B · 4 C · 5 D · 6 A · 7 D · 8 C · 9 C · 10 B.

3. Your ideas + Word Focus · 8 min

"Your ideas": how would you feel if someone from a completely different place wore traditional clothes from your culture? Push for a real opinion, not just "fine" or "bad" — ask why. Word Focus box: status, trademark (v), warriors, pierced (adj), equator, wax, cannon — check these are understood from context, not just translated.

Valencia link: ask whether it would feel different if a tourist wore full Fallera dress (the traditional Valencian festival costume) for a costume party abroad, versus a foreigner living in Valencia wearing it respectfully during Fallas itself — a good real discussion of the same question the book asks.

4. Vocabulary p.20: Exercises 1–2 · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.20, "Old Man Winter" article about the Slovenian Pust festival.

Students read the short article, then match 12 definitions to the bold words in the text. Then Ex.2 pair discussion: legends about your own country's origins; festivals attended, with special costumes; ever taken part in a parade.

Ex.1 answers: 1 tradition · 2 ritual · 3 roles · 4 spectacle · 5 origins · 6 festivities · 7 legend · 8 ceremony · 9 costume · 10 masks · 11 parade · 12 spectators.

5. Vocabulary Exercise 3 + your ideas · 8 min

Pairs discuss what the bold words mean in five sentences (witness, reception, ancestors, bonds, speech) and how far they agree with each. Close with the "your ideas" prompts: is it important to keep old traditions alive, and what ceremonies/traditions from your own country or culture might surprise an outsider?

Ex.3 (meanings): 1 witness — someone who sees an event · 2 reception — a formal event, such as a meal, to celebrate an occasion · 3 ancestors — relatives from the distant past · 4 bonds — connections or links between people · 5 speech — talking in front of an audience.

Valencia link: a strong local example for the "surprise an outsider" question — the Crema (burning of the Fallas monuments on 19 March) genuinely startles first-time visitors, much like the article's bonfire that burns a doll of Pust at the end of the Slovenian festivities.

Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 4 min

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit b2a-u02-l02.

FCE I: free-for-all with three players. Friday B2: teams as usual.

Fast finisher

Write one original sentence using three of today's vocabulary words together (e.g. tradition, ceremony, ancestors) about a real festival, then read it to the group.

If short of time, cut:

The "your ideas" discussion after Ex.3 can be shortened to a single quick show-of-hands question if the exam task discussion ran long. Do not cut the exam task reveal (Block 2) or Ex.1's 12 definitions (Block 4) — both are directly examinable vocabulary and reading-strategy content.
