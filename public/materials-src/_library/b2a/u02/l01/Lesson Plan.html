1. Unit 1 test results · 15 min

Materials: the Unit 1 results PowerPoint (cloned .pptx, _method/level-ladder-results-scripts/), in this date's materials folder; it is built after the Unit 1 test is marked.

Click through as a class: overall scores first (anonymised Student A/B/C), then a slide per section (Reading /6, Vocabulary & Use your English /23, Grammar /23, Listening /8) showing which items the group got right and which need another look (amber “not yet” where fewer than half the class got an item right). Close on the roadmap slide (“YOU ARE HERE”: Unit 1 done, Unit 2 starting today) and the two concrete next steps.

Before class: mark the test, send the marked scans to Claude, who builds the results PowerPoint by cloning the latest FCE I Level Ladder deck (per-question breakdown, anonymised A/B/C cards).

2. Unit opener: p.17 · 7 min

Materials: Student's Book p.17 (photo: a server preparing a crepe in Tokyo, at a stand selling Western-style crepes with Japanese signage).

Pairs discuss the three questions: how the photo shows a mix of cultures; whether you can buy food from other cultures locally; whether borrowing things (food, fashion, words) from other cultures is useful. Take brief whole-group feedback before moving on — this is a taster for the unit theme, not a task to mark.

Valencia link: ask directly — what "mixed-culture" food can you buy in Valencia? (sushi burritos on Calle Cuba, ramen near Ruzafa, the Japanese-Valencian crepe stands that have opened near the Mercado Central are a near-exact parallel to the photo.)

3. Reading lead-in: Exercises 1–3 · 13 min

Materials: Student's Book p.18, Ex.1–3.

Ex.1: pairs discuss how important fashion is to them, what's fashionable locally now, and how fashion has changed in recent decades. Ex.2: find five words in the article (line references given) and complete definitions. Ex.3: look at the four photos on p.19 and say which items (beads, bracelets, earrings, head wraps/turbans, kilt, necklaces, tartan) appear in each.

Ex.2 answers: 1 identity · 2 tribes · 3 lifestyle · 4 reflect · 5 funerals.
Ex.3: a (Maasai woman) — beads, earrings, bracelets, necklaces · b (bagpiper) — a kilt, tartan · c (Nihang warrior) — bracelets, headwraps/turbans · d (ancient fresco) — earrings. Full key on the Vista deck.

4. First read: Exercise 4 · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.19, article “Cultural fashions” (sections A–D).

Students read quickly — general gist, not every word — and complete four sentences identifying the group/place in each photo. Push a fast first read: two minutes, not ten.

Answers: 1 Maasai · 2 Scotland · 3 Nihang · 4 ancient (times).

5. Start the exam task: Exercises 5–6 · 8 min

Materials: Exam Tip box (“Eliminating ‘almost right’ answers”) and Exam Task (10 matching questions, p.18).

Read the Exam Tip together: read the whole article first for gist; underline what each question is really asking; expect “almost right” distractor sections; search for all possible matches before deciding. Students underline the key words in questions 1–10, then start Ex.6 (identifying which section(s) A–D could match each question) — this doesn't need to finish today; l02 completes it and reveals the full key.

Valencia link: before scanning, ask the group: which of the four fashion traditions on p.19 (Maasai beads, Scottish tartan, Nihang turbans, ancient earrings) is most like something from a Valencian or Spanish tradition they know — e.g. the indumentaria valenciana (traditional Fallera dress) worn during Fallas, which like tartan is now mostly reserved for festivals rather than daily wear.

Somerset Arcade: Point Grab

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit b2a-u02-l01.

If time allows at the very end (Friday B2 more likely than FCE I), a short round on today's new vocabulary from the article (status, trademark, warriors, pierced, equator, wax, cannon) and the p.17/18 discussion vocabulary.

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to write one more sentence of their own for each of the five p.18 vocabulary words (identity, tribes, lifestyle, reflect, funerals), about somewhere other than the article.

If short of time, cut:

Ex.3 (photo vocabulary matching) can be done as a quick teacher-led whole-class call-out rather than pair discussion if the results deck ran long. Do not cut the first read (Ex.4) or starting the exam task (Ex.5–6): finishing the actual Exam Task is the point of l02, and it needs somewhere to start from.
