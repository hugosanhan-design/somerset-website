Revision: past tenses, used to and would

About 15 minutes. Do it in pencil, then check with your teacher.
Past simple: finished events, one after another. Past continuous: an action in progress, background, or interrupted by a past simple event.
used to: past habits and states (no longer true). would: past habits only, never states (be, have, like).
1Circle the correct option.
  1. While Nuria (crossed / was crossing) the Plaza del Ayuntamiento, a firework went off.
  2. Jordi (opened / was opening) the door, walked in and sat down.
  3. I (watched / was watching) the mascletà when my phone rang.
  4. When we were children, we (used to go / were going) to the Malvarrosa every Sunday.
  5. My grandmother (would be / used to be) very shy when she was young.
  6. Every summer, Paula (would swim / was swimming) in the sea before breakfast.
  7. While Sergio (cooked / was cooking) the paella, the guests arrived.
  8. Pablo (didn't use to / didn't used to) like horchata.
2Complete with the past simple or past continuous of the verb.
  1. The lights (go out) while we (watch) the fireworks in Las Arenas.
  2. Irene (take) a photo and then (post) it online.
  3. What you (do) at eight o'clock last night?
  4. Daniel (not / hear) the alarm because he (sleep).
  5. Rubén (walk) along the beach when he (see) a dolphin.
  6. Clara (not / go) out because it (rain).

3Write U (used to), W (would) or B (both are possible).
  1. Every August, my family rent a flat in Cullera.
  2. There be a cinema on our street, but it closed.
  3. When I was a child, I have a pet rabbit.
  4. On Saturdays, Nuria get up early and walk along the Turia.
  5. Gemma be afraid of fireworks when she was little.
4Find and correct the mistake. Rewrite the sentence.
  1. I didn't used to like olives.
  2. Yesterday evening I was going to the cinema with Carlos.
  3. When I was small, I would be very shy.
  4. Jordi was breaking his arm while he was playing football.
5Your turn.Write two true sentences.
  1. Something you used to do when you were ten, but don't do now.
  2. What you were doing at eight o'clock yesterday evening.
