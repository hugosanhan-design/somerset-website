What p.16 actually is, checked against the real page image: "Live well, study well" — a life-skills spread on resilience and positive/negative thinking, subtitled "resilience; positive and negative thinking." It is not a video lesson despite the library's placeholder title "Video/unit review" — there is no video component on this page in Unit 1, only a reading, discussion questions, a "Mind your Mind" information box, and two optional Projects (a poster, an action plan). Confirmed against both the Student's Book scan and the Teacher's Book, which lists no video for this page. FCE I currently skips this lesson (Hugo's standing call, matching PET I's rule for the equivalent page) — this plan is written so it also works as a genuine optional/buffer lesson: self-contained, no dependency on surrounding sessions, and droppable without leaving a gap in Unit 1's grammar/vocab/exam coverage, since nothing here is retested on the Unit 1 test.
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can discuss common stressors and use vocabulary and Useful Language for talking about resilience and mental wellbeing, and (unit review) have reviewed Unit 1's key vocabulary and grammar through play and game.
Target language. resilience, cope with, deal with, face a problem, look on the bright side, network, pick yourself up, reward, ashamed, complain, analyse. Discussing problems / being positive Useful Language.
Exam link. None directly — this page is not an exam-task page. It supports Speaking Parts 3–4 (discussion and opinion-giving) more generally.

1. Warm-up: common problems · 5 min

Materials: Student's Book p.16, Exercise 1.

Pairs discuss common problems people their age face: family, friends, school, mental health. Keep it light and general — this is a warm-up, not a counselling session; if anything personal comes up, acknowledge briefly and move the discussion back to the general level.

2. Reading: Building resilience · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.16, Exercises 2–4.

Students read the "Building resilience" box and match explanations 1–4 to tips a–d. Then discuss in pairs: why do we assume other people have perfect lives, and should we be more open about problems? Finally, rank the four tips from easiest to hardest to follow, and justify the choice.

Answers (Ex.2): 1d · 2b · 3a · 4c.

Valencia link: mention that Valencian secondary schools and the Generalitat Valenciana have expanded student wellbeing/orientation programmes (PAT, tutoría) in recent years for exactly the reasons this page discusses (exam stress, Selectividad/EvAU pressure). Ask if students know who their own school's orientador/a is.

3. Mind your Mind + discussion · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.16, Exercises 5–6.

Read the "Mind your Mind" box on positive vs negative thinking together. Discuss: are you generally a positive or negative thinker? Then in pairs, think of real examples of complaining too much, looking on the bright side, and ignoring a problem or risk.

4. Project 1: the positivity poster · 12 min

Materials: paper/board for a quick poster, or done orally if time is short.

In groups (or as a whole three-way discussion at FCE I), list five things people their age often worry about (relationships, studies, work in the future, their own ideas) and find a positive reframe for each. A full poster is optional at this session length — a spoken list with each reframe justified is enough if pressed for time.

5. Unit review: the play · 10 min

Materials: groups/2026-27/fce1/plays/Unit1_JumpForJoy_Play.html (already built — do not rebuild).

Re-run the existing Unit 1 play as a closing review of the unit's emotions/personality vocabulary. Rotate roles if it's been performed before, so each student reads a different part this time.

6. Somerset Arcade: whole-unit Point Grab · 8 min

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit b2a-u01-l08.

This round samples across the whole unit: emotions/personality (p.8), present simple/continuous (p.9), present perfect (p.12), phrasal verbs/prepositions (p.13), and today's resilience vocabulary (p.16) — a genuine "how much of Unit 1 has stuck" check before the test.

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to sketch Project 2 instead (a personal action plan for a real problem they're facing), privately, not shared with the group unless they choose to.

If short of time, cut:

Cut the poster-making itself (Block 4) down to a spoken list, and/or shorten the play re-read to a single scene rather than the whole script. Since this whole lesson is designed as optional/buffer content, any block can be dropped entirely without leaving a gap in the unit's tested material — that is the point of building it this way.
