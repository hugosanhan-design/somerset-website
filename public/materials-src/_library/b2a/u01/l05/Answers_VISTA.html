Present perfect: simple & continuous

Tap "Show answer" next to each question. Six exercises, in book order.

Exercise 1 · matching the contrast

Which sentence answers which question?

  • aFocuses on the result of a finished action → sentence 1 (he's failed his exam)
  • bMentions the result of a process that might still continue → sentence 2 (he's been chopping onions)
  • cStative verb, started in the past, still continues → sentence 3 (I've had this computer for three years)
  • dAction started in the past, still continues → sentence 4 (she's been studying for three years)
  • eFocuses on "how many" → sentence 5 (I've read a lot of books)
  • fFocuses on "how long" → sentence 6 (I've been researching for over a year)
Why: simple = result / a finished count; continuous = a process, possibly still going, or how long it's taken. The REMEMBER box's gone vs been works the same way — gone is a result you're not back from, been is an experience.
Exercise 2 · choose the correct option

Simple or continuous?

  • 1Have you ever eaten sushi?
  • 2It's been raining all day!
  • 3I haven't seen Julia for ages.
  • 4I've been waiting for you since 10 o'clock!
  • 5Sorry, I haven't finished my work.
  • 6This is the first time I've visited Rome.
Pattern: a one-off, completed action or a superlative/"first time" claim → simple. An ongoing, still-continuing activity → continuous.
Exercise 3 · complete the questions

Build the question

  • 1Have you finished your work yet?
  • 2Where has Kim gone? I 've been looking for her all afternoon.
  • 3How long have they been studying English?
  • 4Sorry I'm late. Have you been waiting long?
  • 5Has Luiza started her new job yet?
Note item 2: has gone (result, simple) but 've been looking (the searching is the ongoing process) — both forms can appear in the same exchange because they're doing different jobs.
Exercise 4 · same or different?

What's the real difference?

  • 1Same — but already is stronger ("I don't need to see it again")
  • 2Different — a lot = a large amount; more and more = an increasing amount
  • 3Same
  • 4Same — but still is stronger ("it annoys me it hasn't happened")
  • 5Different — (a) she's read one of the author's books; (b) she's read almost all of them
  • 6Same — but (a) feels very recent; (b), past simple, feels less connected to now
This is the exam-relevant part of the page: B2 First transformations often hinge on exactly this kind of nuance, not just simple/continuous form.
Exercise 5 · adverb position

Where does the adverb go?

  • 1Rachel's just gone out, but she'll be back soon.
  • 2Don't worry if you haven't bought tickets yet.
  • 3I still haven't bought the concert tickets.
  • 4I've been getting more and more anxious lately.
  • 5I've never been so embarrassed before in my life.
  • 6That was the best film I've ever seen.
Rule of thumb: already, just, still, ever, never go before the main verb (after the auxiliary/be); before, lately, recently, yet go at the end.
