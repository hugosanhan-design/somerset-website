Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can choose accurately between the present perfect simple and the present perfect continuous, and can position the adverbs that go with them (already, just, still, yet, ever, never, before, lately, recently) correctly in a sentence.
Target language. Present perfect simple vs continuous (result vs process/duration; "how many" vs "how long"); adverb position.
Exam link. This grammar recurs across Reading, Use of English (Parts 2, 3, 4) and Writing at B2 First — students who confuse simple/continuous here lose marks in open cloze and transformations later in the unit.
60-minute session (FCE I, Tue/Thu group). One grammar page only, so the full hour goes on it — no rushing. Friday B2 fits this together with l06 (Use your English, p.13) in their 2-hour slot: run Blocks 1–4 below (about 40 min), take a 5-minute break, then move straight into the l06 plan (phrasal verbs, prepositions, open cloze exam task, about 45–50 min with its own Point Grab). Do not compress this page to make room — the adverb-position exercises (4–6) are where B2 First candidates typically lose marks, and Friday's extra hour is exactly what buys the room to do both properly.
Materials: Student's Book p.12. Answers_VISTA.html (reveal deck, all six exercises). Audio: Track 1.6 02.mp3 for Exercise 6. Somerset Arcade: b2a-u01-l05.

1. Warm-up: noticing the contrast · 5 min

Materials: board only.

Write on the board: Duncan is upset because he's failed his exam. / Liam is crying because he's been chopping onions. (SB p.12, Ex.1, sentences 1 and 2.) Ask: which one describes a finished result you can see right now (the exam is over, done)? Which one describes something that's been happening, a process, maybe still going on? Don't name "present perfect simple / continuous" yet — let them notice the difference in meaning first.

2. Concept check: Exercise 1 · 7 min

Materials: Student's Book p.12, Exercise 1.

Students read sentences 1–6 in pairs and match them to questions a–f. Go through the answers together and use the REMEMBER box (the two past participles of go: gone for results, been for experiences — Pam's gone to her friend's house vs Pam's been to my house) as a natural bridge into naming the two forms properly.

Answers: a1 · b2 · c4 · d5 (SB shows d as "started in the past and still continues" — the continuous, sentence 4) · e5 · f6. Full key on the Vista deck.

3. Controlled practice: Exercises 2 and 3 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.12, Exercises 2–3. Reveal on Answers_VISTA.html.

Individually, then check in pairs (three students at FCE I compare all together, per the group's standing rule). Exercise 2 is a straight choice between the simple and continuous form; Exercise 3 asks students to build the whole question. Push students to justify each choice out loud before revealing — "why continuous, not simple, here?"

Ex.2: 1 eaten · 2 been raining · 3 seen · 4 been waiting · 5 finished · 6 visited.
Ex.3: 1 Have you finished · 2 has Kim gone / 've been looking · 3 have they been studying · 4 Have you been waiting · 5 Has Luiza started.

4. Adverbs: Exercises 4 and 5 · 18 min

Materials: Student's Book p.12, Exercises 4–5.

Exercise 4 is the harder, more valuable task: six pairs of sentences that look almost identical but carry a real difference in emphasis (already vs just; a lot vs more and more; ever vs never...). Don't let students just tick "same" or "different" without saying why — that reasoning is the actual grammar point tested at B2 First. Exercise 5 is more mechanical: mark where six different adverbs go in a sentence (before the main verb, after the auxiliary, at the end of the clause, etc.).

Ex.4 (possible answers): 1 Same, but already is stronger ("I don't need to see it again"). 2 Different: a lot = large amount; more and more = increasing amount. 3 Same. 4 Same, but still is stronger ("it annoys me it hasn't happened"). 5 Different: a she's read one book; b she's read almost all of them. 6 Same, but a refers to something very recent, b (past simple) feels less connected to now.
Ex.5: 1 Rachel's just gone out… 2 …if you haven't bought tickets yet. 3 I still haven't bought the tickets. 4 I've been getting more and more anxious lately. 5 I've never been so embarrassed before. 6 …the best film I've ever seen.

Valencia link: put a local example on the board for Exercise 4's "how many / how long" contrast — I've visited the Bioparc three times (how many) vs I've been going to the same panadería on my street for ten years (how long, still true). Ask each student for one more example about somewhere in Valencia.

5. Listening check: Exercise 6 · 10 min

Materials: Track 1.6 02.mp3. Student's Book p.12, Exercise 6 (word box: already, before, ever, for, just, never, still, yet).

Students complete the gapped dialogue with the eight adverbs first, from memory/logic, then listen once to check. Play it a second time if needed before revealing.

Answers: 1 for · 2 before · 3 still · 4 yet · 5 never · 6 ever · 7 already · 8 just.

6. Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 8 min

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit b2a-u01-l05.

FCE I: free-for-all with three players (no teams, per the group's standing rule — three is too few to split without leaving someone idle). Friday B2: split into teams as usual. The question bank covers the grammar (form and use) and the adverb rules from today's page.

7. Wrap-up + homework · 2 min

If time is short, skip straight to homework: the "your ideas" speaking prompts at the bottom of p.12 (What have you never done that you'd like to do? What have you been doing more than usual recently?) become a quick pair discussion to close, or a written prep task for next class.

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to write two questions, two affirmative and two negative sentences of their own using the present perfect simple or continuous, then read them to the group (Teacher's Book extension idea for Exercise 2).

If short of time, cut:

Exercise 4's full same/different discussion — keep the "same or different" labels but skip the deeper "why" discussion on 2–3 of the six pairs. Do not cut Exercises 5 or 6: adverb position is the part of this page most likely to appear as a Use of English transformation later in the unit.
