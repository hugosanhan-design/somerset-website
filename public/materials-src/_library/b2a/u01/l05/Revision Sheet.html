Revision: present perfect simple and continuous

About 15 minutes. Do it in pencil, then check with your teacher.
Simple = the result, or how many: I've read three books.
Continuous = the activity, or how long: I've been reading all day.
Stative verbs (have, know, be, like) are never continuous.   gone = he is there now; been = he went and came back.
1Circle the correct option.
  1. Nuria is tired because she (has studied / has been studying) all afternoon.
  2. I (have read / have been reading) three books this month.
  3. How long (have you known / have you been knowing) Pablo?
  4. The streets are wet. It (has rained / has been raining) all morning.
  5. Sorry, I (haven't finished / haven't been finishing) the report yet.
  6. This is the first time I (have visited / have been visiting) Alicante.
  7. Oh no! Someone (has broken / has been breaking) my phone.
  8. The kitchen is so hot because I (have cooked / have been cooking) paella all morning.
2Complete with the present perfect, simple or continuous.
  1. you ever (eat) horchata with fartons?
  2. Where's Carlos? He (wait) outside since half past four.
  3. I (not finish) the book, so don't tell me the ending!
  4. How long they (learn) English?
  5. She (lose) her keys, so she can't get in.
  6. I (know) Irene since primary school.

3Add the adverb in brackets in the best place.Write the new sentence.
  1. She hasn't replied to my message. (yet)
  2. I haven't bought the tickets. (still)
  3. I've seen this film. (already)
  4. It's the best paella I've eaten. (ever)
  5. I've been so tired. (lately)
  6. The bus has arrived. (just)
4Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.
  1. I live in Valencia since 2019.
  2. She has gone to Rome three times.
  3. I have seen him yesterday.
  4. How long are you studying English?
  5. I have been knowing her for years.

  1. Something you have never done but would like to do.
