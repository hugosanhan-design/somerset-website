Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can use seven common phrasal verbs about feelings and relationships, complete four dependent prepositions correctly, and complete a B2 First open cloze exam task, identifying the type of word missing in each gap before guessing.
Target language. Phrasal verbs: go off, cheer up, chill out, hit it off, fall out, get down, stand up. Prepositions: lead to, respond to, concentrate on, responsible for, focus on, burst into.
Exam link. B2 First Reading & Use of English Part 2 (open cloze) — tests exactly this: grammatical words (linkers, prepositions, parts of phrasal verbs), never content vocabulary. One word per gap, spelling counts.
60-minute session (FCE I, Tue/Thu group). Friday B2 runs this straight after l05 (Grammar p.12) in their 2-hour Friday slot — see l05's plan for the join and the timing split (roughly 40 min on p.12, then this page in the remaining time). If you're teaching this on its own hour (FCE I), keep the full 60 minutes; the open cloze exam task at the end deserves real, unhurried exam-condition time, not a rushed five minutes.
Materials: Student's Book p.13. Answers_VISTA.html (reveal deck, three exercises + the exam task). Somerset Arcade: b2a-u01-l06.

1. Warm-up: phrasal verb mime · 5 min

Materials: board only.

Mime "chilling out" (leaning back, relaxed) and ask students to guess. Write chill out on the board. Then say a short story: "My best friend and I had a big argument last week — we've fallen out." Ask what fallen out means from context. This primes both meanings that Exercise 1 will test.

2. Phrasal verbs: Exercise 1 · 13 min

Materials: Student's Book p.13, Exercise 1.

Before students open their books, ask them to define what a phrasal verb is and elicit ones they already know for relationships (get on with, go out with, break up). Then students read sentences 1–7 and match the bold phrasal verbs to definitions a–g in pairs.

Answers: 1b (gone off → stopped liking) · 2d (cheered up → made someone happy) · 3f (chill out → relax) · 4a (hit it off → be friendly with someone) · 5e (fallen out → have an argument) · 6g (getting down → make someone sad) · 7c (stood up → not meet someone you'd arranged to meet).

Teacher's Book tip: phrasal verbs are very common in everyday English and expected at B2. Have students start (or add to) a dedicated phrasal-verb section in their notebooks today.

3. Prepositions: Exercise 2 · 12 min

Materials: Student's Book p.13, Exercise 2.

Students complete six sentences with for, into, on, to in pairs, then discuss how much they personally agree with each sentence — the book explicitly builds this in as a speaking extension, not just a gap-fill.

Answers: 1 to · 2 to · 3 on · 4 for · 5 on · 6 into.

Valencia link: for sentence 4 ("You are responsible for your own happiness"), ask whether students agree, then bring in a local example — e.g. "Some people in Valencia say the Mediterranean lifestyle, long lunches, the beach nearby, makes it easier to focus on happiness than in a bigger, faster city."

4–5. Exam Tip + Exam Task: open cloze · 20 min

Materials: Student's Book p.13, Exam Tip box + Exam Task (8-gap text about Dr Valorie Salimpoor's research on music and emotions).

Read the Exam Tip together first: read the whole text quickly for general meaning; look at the words before and after each gap to decide the type of word missing (linker, preposition, part of a phrasal verb); write exactly one word; check spelling at the end. Students then complete the 8 gaps individually, compare in pairs, and reveal.

Answers: 1 on · 2 on · 3 few · 4 out · 5 Since · 6 are · 7 have · 8 still.

Note for feedback: item 3 ("a few minutes earlier") and item 8 ("there is still a lot we can learn") are the two gaps students most often overthink — remind them the answer is almost always the simplest grammatical word that fits, not a content word.

6. Somerset Arcade: Point Grab · 8 min

Materials: laptop + projector, somerset-arcade/index.html, unit b2a-u01-l06.

FCE I: free-for-all, three players. Friday B2: teams as usual. Covers today's phrasal verbs, prepositions and the open cloze answers.

7. Wrap-up + homework · 2 min

Extension idea from the Teacher's Book: can music influence our emotions? What music do you listen to when you're happy / sad? Quick pair chat to close, or set as homework prep for the writing lesson (pp.14–15), which also deals with emotional register.

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to write one original sentence for each of the seven phrasal verbs from Exercise 1, using a personal example rather than the book's sentence.

If short of time, cut:

The Exercise 2 "how much do you agree" pair discussion — keep the gap-fill answers but skip the discussion. Do not cut the Exam Task or the Exam Tip: open cloze is a full B2 First Use of English part and needs real strategy practice, not just answer-checking.
