Phrasal verbs · Exercise 1

Match the meaning

  • 1gone off him → b, stopped liking
  • 2cheered me up → d, made someone happy
  • 3chill out → f, relax
  • 4hit it off → a, be friendly with someone
  • 5fallen out → e, have an argument
  • 6getting me down → g, make someone sad
  • 7stood me up → c, not meet someone you'd arranged to meet
All seven are common at B2 and above — start a phrasal-verb page in your notebook today, you'll keep adding to it all year.

Exam Task · Open cloze

Valorie Salimpoor and music

Valorie Salimpoor went for a drive that changed her life. She switched 1on the car radio and heard, for the first time in her life, a piece of classical music by Brahms. Valorie recalls feeling a very strong emotion when she heard it. She stopped the car in order to concentrate 2on the music and the positive feelings it was giving her.

Valorie didn't know what was happening. Just a 3few minutes earlier, she had felt sad, but then suddenly had a strong feeling of joy. She knew she had to figure 4out what was going on inside her brain.

5Since her experience in the car, she has conducted experiments into the relationship between music and emotions. She has found that when we're listening to music for the first time, we 6are constantly predicting what we'll hear next, based on all the similar pieces of music we 7have heard before. Our brains then give us a chemical reward when what we hear matches our prediction.

We are just starting to find out about music and the brain, and there is 8still a lot we can learn about how music affects our minds. But there seems to be clear scientific proof that it can cheer us up.
Reminder: open cloze tests grammatical words (linkers, prepositions, phrasal-verb particles), never content vocabulary. Gaps 3 and 8 ("a few minutes"; "there is still a lot") are the two students most often overthink — the answer is nearly always the simplest word that fits.
