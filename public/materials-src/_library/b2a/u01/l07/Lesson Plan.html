Writing is always done in class, never set as homework. The Exam Task at the end of this lesson (an informal reply, 140–190 words) must be written under something close to exam conditions, in the room, where the teacher can see genuine unsupported performance. This is what feeds the error-review lesson (l09).
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can identify formal vs informal tone markers, plan a three/four-paragraph informal email that answers every question asked, and produce a complete B2 First Writing Part 2 informal email under exam-task conditions.
Target language. Informal register: contractions, ellipsis, exclamation marks, chatty connectors (anyway, oh well, how about); useful language for beginning/responding/advising/signing off an informal email.
Exam link. B2 First Writing Part 2, informal letter/email option: 140–190 words, marked on Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation and Language (each /5).
60-minute session (FCE I). This page is genuinely dense — a full Learning Focus, two model texts to read and analyse, a gapped-email exercise, and the exam task itself. At 60 minutes there is real risk of rushing the actual writing. If Blocks 1–4 overrun, protect the writing time (Block 5) at all costs and cut from the model-analysis stage instead (see "if short of time" below). Friday B2 does this together with l08 (p.16 + unit review) in their 2-hour slot: run this full plan first (about 55–60 min, since writing under exam conditions shouldn't be compressed), then l08's lighter, discussion-based content afterward.
Materials: Student's Book pp.14–15. Answers_VISTA.html (reveal deck for the gapped-email exercise and the register exercise). No Point Grab for this lesson — writing is produced, not drilled; see BUILD_REPORT.md.

1. Learning Focus: using the correct tone · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.14, Learning Focus box.

Ask students how they'd start an email to a friend vs a job application; write two columns on the board (informal: Hi, how are you? Lots of love... vs formal: Dear Sir/Madam, Yours faithfully). Then read the six informal-tone techniques in the box together: friendly openers/closers, contractions, personal pronouns, ellipsis, controlled use of exclamation marks, chatty connectors. Flag explicitly: no text-speak (LOL) even in an informal exam answer.

2. Register check: Exercise 1 · 6 min

Materials: Student's Book p.14, Exercise 1.
Answers: 1 F (regret; full form cannot) · 2 I (subject omitted) · 3 F (full form is not) · 4 I (oh well; can't) · 5 I (anyway, how about) · 6 F (unfortunately; full form have been sold) · 7 F (grateful, assistance instead of thanks, help) · 8 I (I'm) · 9 I (great) · 10 I (auxiliary absent; got instead of have).

3. Model reading: Exercises 2–5 · 12 min

Materials: Student's Book p.14 (Tom's email, Emma's reply), p.15 Exercises 4–5.

Read Tom's email (annoyed his friend David has invited extra people camping) and discuss in pairs whether he's right to be annoyed. Read Emma's reply and discuss the advice. Then Exercise 4: which paragraphs directly answer Tom's question, and which are more about being friendly? Exercise 5: underline three phrases for giving advice.

Ex.4 answers: 1 The 2nd and 3rd paragraphs ("As for your friend David..."; "If I were you...") answer the question. 2 The 1st and last paragraphs are more about being friendly.
Ex.5: I don't think you should...; If I were you, I'd...; Why don't you...?

4. Controlled practice: Exercise 6 · 7 min

Materials: Student's Book p.15, Exercise 6 + Useful Language box.
Answers: 1 Hi/Hello · 2 Thanks · 3 hear · 4 sorry · 5 sounds · 6 must · 7 were · 8 don't · 9 even · 10 hope · 11 know · 12 best.

Fast finisher / extension: underline the phrasal verbs in Amy's email (fall out, give up on, chill out, invite over, get on) — a nice link back to l06's phrasal verb page.

5. Exam Tip + plan: Exercise 7 · 5 min

Materials: Student's Book p.15, Exam Tip + Exercise 7.

Planning tip: spend 5–10 minutes on a paragraph plan before writing; include 3–4 short paragraphs; answer every question the friend asked; spend ~25 minutes writing and 5 checking. In pairs, sketch a paragraph plan for the real Exam Task below (Kelly's email about her annoying cousin Kevin) before moving to Block 6.

6. Exam Task: the reply · 18 min

Materials: Student's Book p.15, Exam Task (Kelly's email, 140–190 words).

Task as printed: "You have received this email from your English-speaking friend, Kelly. Guess what? My cousin Kevin has come to stay for the summer and he's incredibly annoying! He's three years younger than me, but he's so childish! He's always singing silly songs or talking to me when I'm busy! I know he's only trying to be friendly, but he's driving me crazy! What should I do? Any ideas? Write soon, Kelly." Students write their reply individually and in silence, following their plan and using the Useful Language from p.15. Teacher times it like the real exam (about 25 minutes) and circulates without correcting live.

Valencia link: if a student is stuck for advice content, suggest local, concrete ideas Kelly's friend could offer Kevin: a trip to the Ciutat de les Arts i les Ciències, an afternoon at the beach, or joining a local football game — genuine, specific suggestions score better than generic advice.

7. Reflection + collect · 4 min

Materials: Student's Book p.15, Reflection Checklist (Exercise 9).

Students tick the five checklist items against their own writing (friendly opener/closer, answered every question, clear paragraphs, appropriate tone throughout, checked carefully). Collect all scripts — this is the raw material for l09, the writing error-review lesson. Do not return or correct anything today.

Fast finisher

A student who finishes early and has genuinely checked their work (not just stopped) can swap papers with a partner and tick the Reflection Checklist against their partner's writing — the book's own extension idea.

If short of time, cut:

Compress Block 3 (model reading) to the two comprehension questions only, skipping the deeper "which paragraphs do what" analysis (Exercise 4) if you must. Never cut or shorten Block 6, the actual writing — this is the entire point of the lesson and the source material for l09.
