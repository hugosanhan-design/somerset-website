FCE I · Friday B2 (paired with l04)
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can choose accurately between will, going to, shall and should for predictions, decisions, plans, offers and suggestions, and can use the future continuous, future perfect simple and future perfect continuous correctly, including after time words like before/after/when/until.
Target language. Future forms: will / going to / shall / should; future continuous; future perfect simple; future perfect continuous; no will/going to after before/after/when/until.
Exam link. Future forms are tested throughout B2 First Use of English (Parts 3–4) and in narrative/planning contexts across Reading, Listening and Writing.
60-minute session (FCE I). One dense grammar page, six exercises, full hour. Friday B2 pairs this with l04 (Listening p.46 + Speaking p.47) in their 2-hour slot: run Blocks 1–6 below (about 50 min), break, then l04's plan (about 60 min with its own Point Grab).
Materials: Student's Book p.45. Track 4.2. Answers_VISTA.html. Somerset Arcade: b2a-u04-l03.

1–2. Listening + underlining: Exercises 1–2 · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.45, Ex.1–2, Track 4.2.

Ex.1: listen and decide who (Isaac or Gabby) has already made plans, who makes a decision, who makes a promise, during the conversation. Ex.2: underline the future forms in the eight numbered sentences.

Ex.1 answers: 1 Gabby (has already made plans for tomorrow) · 2 Isaac (makes a decision) · 3 Isaac (makes a promise).
Ex.2 (forms to underline): 1 Shall we go · 2 is going to be · 3 It will be · 4 we are all going to set off · 5 It should look · 6 I will join · 7 I will send · 8 I will not be.

3. Complete the rules: Exercises 3–4 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.45, Ex.3–4.
Ex.3 answers: a will (sure predictions, opinion) / going to (predictions based on evidence) · b will (decision) / going to (plans made earlier) · c will (offers and promises) · d shall (suggestions with I/we) · e should (predictions when not sure, instead of will).
Ex.4 (match sentence to rule): 1c (future continuous: situations in progress around a future time) · 2d (no will/going to after before/after/when/until) · 3a (future perfect simple: completed before a future time) · 4b (future perfect continuous: over a period, with future results).

4. Practice: Exercise 5 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.45, Ex.5 (eight short exchanges, choose the correct option).
Answers: 1 shall (suggestion, no strong opinion given) · 2 will (prediction, hopeful opinion) · 3 should (less certain than will) · 4 will (confident, based on habit/evidence) · 5 be eating (future continuous: in progress at six o'clock) · 6 have eaten (future perfect: finished by seven) · 7 been studying (future perfect continuous: over the whole day) · 8 finished (future perfect simple: a completed state, exams over).

5. Transformation: Exercise 6 · 15 min

Materials: Student's Book p.45, Ex.6 (rewrite using the word given).
Answers:
1 I'll message you when we finish shopping (or: when we've finished shopping) — hopefully soon.
2 By the time we get to the station, the train will have left.
3 You can't go until the light changes (or: until the light has changed) to green.
4 There are lots of maps, so you shouldn't get lost.
5 I'll be waiting for you outside the train station when you arrive.
6 By the time they finish that skyscraper, they will have been working on it for four years.

Valencia link: practise item 5's pattern with a real example: "By the time the new Ciutat de les Arts i les Ciències extension opens, workers will have been building it for years" — ask students to invent a similarly structured sentence about a real building project in Valencia they know of.

Fast finisher

Write three sentences about your own weekend plans: one using going to (already decided), one using will (a promise or offer), one using the future perfect (something that will be finished by Sunday night).

If short of time, cut:

Exercise 4 (matching sentences to rules) can be done as fast teacher-led feedback rather than pair discussion if Ex.1–2 ran long. Do not cut Ex.6: the transformation practice, especially the before/after/when/until rule, is the single most common B2 First Use of English error with this grammar point.
