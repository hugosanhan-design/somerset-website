Aims. By the end of the lesson, students have understood a real research-interview video on cycling and air pollution well enough to complete true/false and gapped-summary tasks, and have consolidated the whole unit's vocabulary and grammar through the class play and a full-unit Point Grab round.
Target language. Video vocabulary (zone, concentrations, dose, monitors, lungs, route); recap of the whole unit (city vocabulary, future forms, countable/uncountable, quantifiers).
Exam link. B2 First Listening-style gist and detail work; the Review page doubles as informal revision before the Unit 4 test (l10).

2–3. The video: Exercises 2–3 · 21 min

Materials: Student's Book p.52, video "Could biking in a city be bad for you?" Answers checked against the printed SB answer key and the Teacher's Book audioscript, never guessed.
Ex.2 (true/false): 1 F (Steven Chillrud does not say people shouldn't cycle in polluted areas — the question is whether it's worth it) · 2 F (motor vehicles have become cleaner over the last 20–30 years, though still not clean enough) · 3 F (the main goal is to measure and understand pollution exposure, not directly to reduce it) · 4 T (if successful, they get the green light to do a larger health study) · 5 F (particles from diesel vehicles are a particular concern, not "safer") · 6 T (the goal is a phone app to help cyclists choose a route that minimises pollution exposure).
Ex.3 (summary gaps): 1 zone · 2 concentrations · 3 dose · 4 monitors · 5 lungs · 6 route.

Valencia link: Valencia has an extensive, mostly-segregated bike lane network (the Anillo Ciclista and radial routes) that keeps cyclists further from traffic exhaust than the shared London streets in the video — ask the group whether they think Valencia's cyclists breathe less polluted air than the ones the researchers studied.

4–5. Review p.80 (SB p.152) · 12 min

Materials: Review, Unit 4 page (vocabulary + grammar recap, printed on p.80 of this Split A edition; the official SB numbering is p.152).
Vocabulary Ex.1 (complete the words): 1 urban · 2 venue · 3 junction · 4 rural · 5 inhabitants · 6 suburbs.
Ex.2 (compound nouns): 1 housing estate · 2 bike lane · 3 traffic lights · 4 speed limit · 5 crossroads · 6 roadworks.
Ex.3 (matching): 1e 2f 3g 4a 5d 6b 7c 8h.
Grammar Ex.4 (future forms): 1 I'm going · 2 Shall · 3 you'll · 4 should · 5 I'll.
Ex.5 (find and correct the mistake): 1 "will have been going" should be will have gone · 2 "until I'll know" should be until I know · 3 "we've already eaten" should be we'll have already eaten · 4 "You'll be recognising" should be You'll recognise · 5 "after I'll get" should be after I get.
Ex.6 (quantifiers): 1 several · 2 a bit · 3 much · 4 Few · 5 every · 6 a little · 7 competition · 8 experience.

6. Class play

Materials: groups/2026-27/fce1/plays/Unit4_CityLiving_Play.html (already built, reused as-is — do not rebuild).

Pick one of the play's three scripts (2/3-character toggle). Assign parts, read through once for meaning, then again with more expression. Covers the unit's vocabulary and grammar in a Valencia/Spain setting of its own.

If short of time, cut:

The Review page's grammar recap (Block 5) is the first thing to shorten to a quick spot-check of 2–3 items rather than all three exercises, since l10's test itself is the real, marked check. Never cut the video Exam Task (Ex.2–3): a real video-comprehension mark only comes from that.
