New Close-up B2 Split A, Unit 4 · City living
FCE I · Friday B2 (paired with l06)
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can identify countable and uncountable nouns (including words that change meaning depending on countability), and can use quantifiers (few/little, many/much, all/no/several/plenty/most/every/a few of) correctly.
Target language. Countable/uncountable nouns; quantifiers (few, little, many, much, all, no, several, plenty, most, every, a few of).
Exam link. Quantifiers and noun countability are tested throughout B2 First Use of English (Parts 1–4) and are a very common accuracy error at this level.
60-minute session (FCE I). Friday B2 pairs this with l06 (Use your English p.49) in their 2-hour slot: run Blocks 1–4 below (about 40 min), break, then l06's plan (about 60 min with its own Point Grab).
Materials: Student's Book p.48. Answers_VISTA.html. Somerset Arcade: b2a-u04-l05.

1. Countable/uncountable: Exercise 1 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.48, Ex.1 (paragraph about a changing city).
Answers: Countable — years, city, people, cars, roads, inhabitants. Uncountable — rubbish, traffic, pollution, air, stress, health.

Remind the group of the REMEMBER box: a few uncountable nouns end in -s (news, maths, politics) but still take a singular verb; some nouns are plural-only (clothes, outskirts, police); some words that are countable in Spanish (money, equipment, furniture) are uncountable in English.

2. Same word, different meaning: Exercise 2 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.48, Ex.2 (five word pairs).
Answers: 1a experience (uncountable: knowledge/skill built up over time) / 1b experience (countable: a single event) · 2a spaces (countable: available places) / 2b space (uncountable: room) · 3a light (uncountable: illumination) / 3b lights (countable: light fittings/lamps) · 4a competition (uncountable: rivalry) / 4b competition (countable: a contest) · 5a work (uncountable: tasks/employment) / 5b works (countable: pieces, e.g. of art).

3. Quantifier rules: Exercise 3 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.48, Ex.3.
Answers: a Few and many can only be used with countable nouns. b Little and much can only be used with uncountable nouns.

4. Bogotá text: Exercise 4 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.48, Ex.4 (eight-gap text on the Ciclovía in Bogotá, Colombia).
Answers: 1 Every · 2 many · 3 a few of · 4 All · 5 no · 6 plenty · 7 most · 8 several.

Valencia link: Bogotá's Ciclovía closes streets to cars every Sunday morning — ask the group whether they know Valencia's own closest equivalent (the Anillo Ciclista bike ring around the old city walls, or the car-free Turia Gardens riverbed) and whether a full weekly Ciclovía-style street closure would work here.

Fast finisher

Write two true sentences about your own city using a quantifier each: one with few/many (countable), one with little/much (uncountable).

If short of time, cut:

Exercise 2 (the five word-pairs) can be shortened to three pairs rather than all five if Ex.1 discussion ran long. Do not cut Ex.4: it is the only exercise that puts every quantifier from the page into connected, realistic text.
