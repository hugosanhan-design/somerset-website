Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can eliminate clearly wrong options before listening again for detail (Listening Part 3-style multiple choice), and can express opinions, agree/disagree/partly agree and complete a collaborative decision-making task (Speaking Part 4-style).
Target language. City/health vocabulary (city-dweller, noise pollution, homesick, mood swings); agreeing/disagreeing/partly agreeing phrases.
Exam link. B2 First Listening Part 3 (multiple choice, elimination technique) and Speaking Part 4 (collaborative task, decision-making).
60-minute session (FCE I). Friday B2 pairs this with l03 in their 2-hour slot, running after it.

1. Vocabulary check: Exercise 1 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.46, Ex.1 (eight sentences, choose the correct option).
Answers: 1 away from · 2 city-dweller · 3 pollution · 4 controls it · 5 often · 6 design towns and cities · 7 change sharply and frequently · 8 miss your home.

3. First listen + eliminate: Exercises 2–3 · 12 min

Materials: Student's Book p.46, Ex.2–3, Track 4.3 (two speakers discussing Brighton and cities).
Answers: 1 C, a city by the sea (not A: Brighton is not rural; not B: it is not inland) · 2 B, he would rather live somewhere quieter (not A: he does not mention employment; not C: he does not like the sea specifically) · 3 C, people from different backgrounds (not A: not just people from her hometown; not B: not mainly local people).

4. Exam Task: Exercise 4 · 13 min

Materials: Student's Book p.46, Exam Task questions 1–7, Track 4.4 (interview with psychologist Karen Black).
Answers: 1 C (the majority of people will live in cities by 2050) · 2 A (city people lead more stressed lives) · 3 A (crime is rising in cities) · 4 B (suburb-dwellers are negatively affected by city stress) · 5 A (people's brains work differently depending on where they live) · 6 B (town planners) · 7 C (she enjoys the things that make cities harmful).

Valencia link: ask the group afterwards: does Valencia feel like the "stressful" city Karen describes, or is it closer to a suburb in terms of pace of life? Where would they place it on her scale?

6. Speaking model: Exercises 2–3 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.47, Ex.2–3, Track 4.5.

Listen to two speakers discussing the same five topics as Ex.1 (parks/green spaces, residential areas, shopping, transport, work) and note where they completely agree, completely disagree, and partly agree.

Ex.3 answers: 1 They completely agree on shopping and residential areas. 2 They completely disagree on transport, parks and green spaces. 3 They partly agree on workplaces.

7. Collaborative task: Exercise 4 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.47, Exam Task (five ideas: better public transport, more shopping centres, more parks and green space, more car-free zones, more entertainment facilities). Useful Language box (agreeing/disagreeing/partly agreeing).

Pairs spend about two minutes discussing how each idea would improve people's lives, then one minute deciding which idea would help the most — timed exactly as in the real exam.

Valencia link: before the timed task, ask which of the five ideas Valencia has already tried (e.g. more car-free zones — the pedestrianised city centre and the Anillo Ciclista bike ring) and whether students think it worked.

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to rank the five collaborative-task ideas from most to least useful for Valencia specifically, giving one reason for each.

If short of time, cut:

Speaking Ex.2–3 (listening to the model conversation) can be shortened to a single listen rather than listen-and-check twice if the Listening Exam Task (Block 4) ran long. Do not cut the timed collaborative task (Block 7): the two-minutes-plus-one-minute structure is exactly how B2 First Speaking Part 4 is timed in the real exam.
