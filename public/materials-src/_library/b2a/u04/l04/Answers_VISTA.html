Exercises 2–3 · eliminate then choose

Brighton, a city by the sea

  • 1The woman says Brighton is → C, a city by the sea.
  • 2The man does not like cities because → B, he would rather live somewhere quieter.
  • 3The woman has met → C, people from different backgrounds.
Why the others are wrong (from the key): Q1 — B is wrong: the woman says she would ‘hate to live in a city further inland’. Q2 — A is wrong: the man says he found a job in the city. Q3 — A is wrong: the woman doesn’t say anything about people from her hometown.
Exercise 4 · Exam Task, Karen Black interview

Cities, health and the brain

  • 1By 2050 → C, the majority of people will live in cities.
  • 2City people → A, lead more stressed lives.
  • 3In cities → A, crime is rising.
  • 4Suburb-dwellers → B, are negatively affected by the stress of the city.
  • 5Karen's research has proved → A, people's brains work differently depending on where they live.
  • 6The results might be useful to → B, town planners.
  • 7Karen is happy to live in a city because → C, she enjoys the things that make it harmful.
Speaking Ex.3 · the model conversation

What do they agree on?

  • 1Completely agree on → shopping and residential areas.
  • 2Completely disagree on → transport, parks and green spaces.
  • 3Partly agree on → workplaces.
Speaking Ex.4 · collaborative task

Five ideas, one decision

  • •Discuss: better public transport, more shopping centres, more parks and green space, more car-free zones, more entertainment facilities.
  • •Two minutes on the main discussion, one minute reaching a decision — timed exactly as in the exam.
  • •Use the Useful Language: agreeing (I couldn't agree more that…), disagreeing (I'm afraid I don't agree…), partly agreeing (You're right…, but…).
No single answer: this is a discussion task, not a right/wrong exercise — the goal is using the Useful Language naturally while reaching a genuine group decision.
