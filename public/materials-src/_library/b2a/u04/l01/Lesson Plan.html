Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can discuss what city living involves, use five article-based words in context (identical, urban, rapid, residents, commercial), and have identified which person/place each of the four Exam Task sections is mainly about, ready to complete the exam task in l02.
Target language. City vocabulary from the article (identical, urban, rapid, residents, commercial, humanistic, commuter, gated community, unfenced).
Exam link. B2 First Reading multiple matching (Part 7): the Exam Tip's non-linear reading technique is introduced here and used properly in l02.

2. Reading lead-in: Exercises 1–2 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.42, Ex.1–2.

Ex.1: pairs discuss whether they would rather live in a big city or a rural area, and why. Ex.2: find five words in the article on p.43 (line references given) and use them to complete four sentences.

Ex.2 answers: 1 identical · 2 urban · 3 rapid · 4 Residents, commercial.

3. Discuss and personalise: Exercises 3–4 · 10 min

Materials: Student's Book p.42, Ex.3–4.

Ex.3: do students agree with the four statements in Ex.2? Ex.4: pairs think of a city they know and answer six questions about it (inhabitants, architecture, green space, public transport, walkability, housing) — genuine discussion, no fixed answer.

Valencia link: ask the group to answer Ex.4's six questions about Valencia itself first, before choosing another city — how walkable is the centre compared with the outskirts? What is Valencia's own equivalent of a "green space" (the old riverbed, now the Turia Gardens, running the length of the city)?

4. First read + Exam Tip: Exercises 5–6 · 20 min

Materials: Student's Book p.42 (Exam Tip, Exam Task questions 1–10) and p.43, "Building the cities of the future" (sections A–D).

Read the Exam Tip together: non-linear reading, reading the questions first, spending a few seconds per section for a general idea, matching easy ones quickly, then the rest more carefully. Students underline the key words in questions 1–10 (Ex.5), then read the four sections quickly and say which person/place each is mainly about (Ex.6) — this doesn't need to finish today; l02 completes the actual matching (Ex.7) and reveals the full key.

Ex.6 answers: A Silicon Valley, California — Peter Calthorpe (urban designer). B Paris — Le Corbusier (modernist architect). C Copenhagen — Jan Gehl (urban designer). D China — Xiong'an New Area.

Valencia link: before matching starts, ask the group which of the four cities/approaches (Silicon Valley's car-dependent sprawl, Le Corbusier's tower blocks, Copenhagen's cycling culture, China's "superblocks") is closest to how Valencia is actually built — most will say Copenhagen, given Valencia's own bike-lane network and the car-free Turia Gardens.

Fast finisher

Ask fast finishers to write one more sentence of their own for each of the five p.42 vocabulary words (identical, urban, rapid, residents, commercial), about a city other than the one discussed.

If short of time, cut:

Exercise 4 (personalised city discussion) can be shortened to two questions rather than all six if the opener discussion (Block 1) ran long. Do not cut the Exam Tip or Ex.6: identifying what each section is about is the whole basis for l02's matching task.
