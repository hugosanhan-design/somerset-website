Lesson Plan · b2a-u04-l07
New Close-up B2 Split A, Unit 4 · City living
FCE I · Friday B2 (paired with l08)
Aims. By the end of the lesson, students can engage a reader using rhetorical questions and a personal style, plan an article that answers both parts of a task, and write a real 140–190-word article in class.
Target language. Engaging-opener phrases (Imagine this…, Have you ever found yourself…), rhetorical questions, advice language (Remember that…, You may think that…, but…).
Exam link. B2 First Writing Part 2, article. Writing is always done in class, never as homework (house rule).
60-minute session (FCE I). Friday B2 pairs this with l08 (Video + Unit Review) in their 2-hour slot: run this plan first (about 60 min), break, then l08 (about 60 min).
Materials: Student's Book pp.50-51. Answers_VISTA.html. Blank paper/laptops for the timed writing. Somerset Arcade: b2a-u04-l07.

1. Learning Focus + Exercise 1 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.50, Learning Focus box, Ex.1 (six statements).
Ex.1 answers: 1 essays · 2 articles · 3 essays · 4 articles · 5 articles · 6 essays.

3. Compare openers: Exercise 3 · 7 min

Materials: Student's Book p.50, Ex.3 (two introduction paragraphs, a and b).
Answer: Paragraph b is better: it engages the reader by imagining their situation, addresses them directly as "you", and ends with a rhetorical question that makes the reader want to keep reading. Paragraph a copies too much language from the question, is too formal for an article (however, furthermore), and reads like an essay rather than advice.

4. Model analysis: Exercises 4–5 · 8 min

Materials: Student's Book p.50, the rest of the student's model answer (paragraphs 2–4).
Ex.4 answers: Paragraph 2 — answers the first part of the task (managing your time). Paragraph 3 — answers the second part (managing your money). Paragraph 4 — conclusion.
Ex.5 answers: 1 Yes, both parts of the task are covered, in paragraphs 2 and 3. 2 Yes — almost every sentence contains "you" (or an imperative) and the points are relevant to students.

5. Useful Language: Exercise 6 · 6 min

Materials: Student's Book p.51, Ex.6 (eight-gap extract) and the Useful Language box.
Answers: 1 Imagine · 2 supposed · 3 solution · 4 fact · 5 think · 6 may · 7 remember · 8 surprising.

7. Exam Task: Exercise 8 · 16 min

Materials: Student's Book p.51, Exam Task — "A NEW LIFE!": what advice would you give to a young person who has just moved to the place you live? What mistakes should they avoid? 140–190 words.

Students write individually, in class, using their plan from Ex.7 and the Useful Language box. Collect every script at the end — these are marked before l09 (writing error-review), never returned unmarked and never set as homework.

Valencia link: the task is already Valencia-relevant for students who live here — encourage real local advice and real local mistakes to avoid (e.g. parking rules during Fallas, how the metro/EMT bus system actually works, which neighbourhoods are walkable) rather than generic "a city" answers.

If short of time, cut:

Exercise 3 (comparing the two openers) can be done as fast whole-class feedback rather than pair discussion if Ex.1–2 ran long. Never cut or shorten Block 7 (the real Exam Task): the actual 140–190-word article, written in class, is the whole point of the lesson and the input for l09.
