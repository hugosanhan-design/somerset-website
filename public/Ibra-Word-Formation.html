Part 1 · The rules of thumb

1Ask four questions at every gap

Word formation is not guessing — it's a checklist. Before you write anything, run these four questions. They catch almost every mistake you made in the mock.

  1. What type of word does the gap need? Noun, verb, adjective or adverb? Look at the words around the gap, not the gap itself.
  2. Positive or negative meaning? Does the sentence need a prefix like un-, in-, dis-? (e.g. "It was impossible.")
  3. If it's a noun — singular or plural? Countable? "a great deal of similarity" (uncountable) vs "many similarities".
  4. Check the spelling. A right word with a wrong spelling scores zero. This is where you lost marks.

2How to tell which type of word the gap needs

Question 1 is the most important, so here are the signals. The words touching the gap tell you the answer.

You need a…when the gap is…example
Nounafter a / an / the, after an adjective, or after a preposition (of, in, for)a great decision · lots of information
Adjectivebefore a noun, or after be / seem / look / becomea creative team · it was useful
Adverbdescribing a verb or an adjective (usually ends -ly)she sang beautifully · completely new
Verbafter to, after a modal (can, will), or after a subjectit will strengthen the team

3The endings (suffixes) worth knowing by heart

Make a…Common endingsExamples
Noun (thing/idea)-tion/-sion · -ment · -ity · -ness · -ance/-encedecision, development, ability, happiness, importance
Noun (person)-er · -or · -ist · -antteacher, actor, economist, assistant
Adjective-ive · -ous · -al/-ical · -able/-ible · -ent/-antcreative, famous, natural, comfortable, different
Adjective (with/without)-ful = with · -less = withoutcareful (with care) · hopeless (without hope)
Adverb-lyquickly, easily, dramatically
Verb-ise/-ize · -ify · -enmodernise, simplify, strengthen
Spelling changes to watch: y → i (happy → happiness, rely → reliable) · drop the silent e (create → creation) · -ic → -ically (dramatic → dramatically) · and the one that caught you: many adjectives end -tial, not "-cial" → influential, essential.

4Negative prefixes — a sound rule, not random

Which negative prefix? It's mostly decided by the first letter of the word, to make it easier to say:

PrefixRule of thumbExamples
il-before a word starting with lillegal, illogical
ir-before a word starting with rirregular, irresponsible
im-before m, p (and b)impossible, impatient
in-most other casesincorrect, invisible
un- / dis- / mis-very common; learn per wordunhappy, disagree, misunderstand

⚠ Your four from the mock — learn these cold

  • INFLUENCE → influential — adjective, ends -tial (not "influencial").
  • CREATE → creative — before a noun you need the adjective, not the noun "creation".
  • SIMILAR → similarity — after "a great deal of" the noun is uncountable (not "similarities").
  • ECONOMY → economists — "according to ___" needs the people, and here it's plural.
